Exposed AI Runtime Probing and Callbacks

High severity TLP:CLEAR Part 1 of 2 Research by Rapid7 Published 19 Nov 2026

Hypothesis

An adversary is identifying unauthenticated AI runtimes exposed to the internet and using them to verify connectivity or establish command-and-control via outbound tunneling services.

Brief

Why Now

Rapid7 recently published Open by Default, Already Occupied: Threat Actors are Renting your Compute, detailing how adversaries exploit unauthenticated AI runtimes. Thousands of instances of Ollama and Jupyter are currently exposed to the internet without a password. Attackers do not just want the data; they want the compute. They use these systems to run their own models or establish persistent outbound tunnels to bypass firewalls.

How the Hunt Flows

The hunt begins with a scoping phase on the hb_exposed_assets surface. It identifies internal hosts that external scanners have already flagged as exposing AI runtimes. This focuses the investigation on the most likely entry points rather than searching every host in the estate.

Once the hunt identifies exposed hosts, it pivots to behavioral telemetry. The workflow runs two queries in parallel. The first checks the hb_dns_activity surface for resolutions of free tunneling domains like bore.pub or trycloudflare.com. These services are commonly used by attackers to create a stable callback from an unauthenticated server. The second query monitors the hb_http_activity surface for probes against sensitive API paths, such as /api/pull or /api/tags, coming from external IP addresses.

Finally, an analyst or agent triage weighs these signals. A host that appears in the external exposure list and also initiates outbound tunnel DNS lookups suggests a successful compromise. This correlation allows the team to distinguish between a service that is merely misconfigured and one that is actively being used as an adversary's remote terminal.

What the Hunt Cannot See

This hunt has two primary blind spots. First, it cannot inspect the full HTTP request body in many standard proxy logs. An attacker might use a POST request to /api/pull containing a path-traversal payload; while we see the request to the endpoint, we may not see the specific malicious manifest digest. Second, unmanaged VPS hosts often lack process-to-network mapping. Without this, we cannot definitively prove the Ollama process initiated the DNS resolution rather than a legitimate developer tool.

Steps

  1. Query, scoping

    Identify exposed AI services

    Find hosts that external scanners have already flagged as exposing AI runtimes or related ports.

    sql, reads hb_exposed_assets
    SELECT domain_or_ip, port, product, providerFROM hb_exposed_assetsWHERE (LOWER(product) LIKE '%ollama%' OR LOWER(product) LIKE '%jupyter%' OR port IN (11434, 8888, 8501, 8000))

    What a hit looks like. Rows naming external IPs and ports of known AI runtimes. Silence suggests no current exposure detected by external scanning.

  2. Query, baseline

    DNS resolution for tunnel services

    Detect internal hosts resolving tunnel providers which indicates a successful callback or verification check.

    sql, reads hb_dns_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, query_hostname, COUNT(*) AS lookups, MIN(time) AS first_seenFROM hb_dns_activityWHERE instr(',' || '{{tunnel_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(query_hostname) || ',') > 0  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY device_hostname, query_hostname

    What a hit looks like. Internal hosts attempting to connect to free tunnel providers. Silence means no known tunnel callbacks were initiated.

  3. Query, detection candidate

    Unauthenticated AI API probing

    Identify external requests to sensitive endpoints that expose server state or model information across several runtimes.

    sql, reads hb_http_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, url_path, src_endpoint_ip, status_code, timeFROM hb_http_activityWHERE (LOWER(url_path) LIKE '/api/tags%' OR LOWER(url_path) LIKE '/api/pull%' OR LOWER(url_path) LIKE '/api/push%' OR LOWER(url_path) LIKE '/api/kernels%' OR LOWER(url_path) LIKE '/api/contents%' OR LOWER(url_path) LIKE '/api/sessions%')  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. HTTP requests from external IPs to administrative paths. Silence suggests no unauthenticated probing was logged.

  4. Agent triage

    Weigh exposure and callbacks

    Determine if the unauthenticated exposure and subsequent tunnel activity indicate a successful exploitation.

  5. Decision

    Route on verdict

    Direct the hunt based on the agent's assessment of compromise.

  6. Response action

    Isolate compromised AI host

    Stop further misuse or data exfiltration from the compromised runtime.

  7. Analyst task

    Manual forensic review

    Identify specific model names or encoded payloads that indicate the attacker's intent.

  8. Analyst task

    Remediation and hardening

    Close the exposure gap for hosts that were not actively misused.

# Exposed AI Runtime Probing and Callbacks

This hunt identifies AI runtimes like Ollama and Jupyter exposed to the public internet without authentication. It then searches for evidence of active misuse by correlating this exposure with outbound DNS callbacks to free tunneling services and suspicious HTTP probes against sensitive API endpoints. By combining external exposure data with internal telemetry, the hunt distinguishes between simple misconfiguration and active server-side request forgery (SSRF) or persistence attempts.

## exposed-ai-scoping
<!-- Identify exposed AI services -->
Find hosts that external scanners have already flagged as exposing AI runtimes or related ports.

```sqlite target=endpoint role=scoping
~~~yaml

- domain_or_ip
- port
- product
- provider
silence: not_evidence_of_absence
```

## decide-compromise
<!-- Route on verdict -->
if~: "the triage-ai-exposure verdict is malicious for at least one host" (confidence: high, judge=hunter)
then: → isolate-host
indeterminate: → manual-forensics
unavailable: → manual-forensics (blind_spot: missing-http-request-bodies)
else: → remediation-hardening

## isolate-host
<!-- Isolate compromised AI host -->
```action target=endpoint
~~~yaml
approval: required
~~~
Isolate the host immediately. Revoke any linked Ollama account keys and check for unauthorized cloud spend.
```
→ manual-forensics

## manual-forensics
<!-- Manual forensic review -->
```manual target=analyst
Read the model list on the affected host using the Ollama or Jupyter API. Look for model names prefixed with IP addresses or containing Base64 strings that decode to system paths.
```
→ end

## remediation-hardening
<!-- Remediation and hardening -->
```manual target=analyst
Bind the AI runtime to localhost or a private network. Implement an authenticating reverse proxy or zero-trust layer for remote access.
```
→ end
