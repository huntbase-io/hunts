VPN Entry and Identity Harvest

High severity TLP:CLEAR Research by Huntress Published 4 Oct 2026

Hypothesis

An adversary has gained initial access via a VPN without multi-factor authentication and is harvesting credentials via LSASS dumping or Kerberoasting to facilitate exfiltration and eventual disk encryption.

Brief

Why this hunt

In the article "The First 24 Hours: What Happens When Ransomware Lands" (https://www.huntress.com/blog/what-happens-during-a-ransomware-attack), Huntress describes the rapid progression from initial access to full estate encryption. We designed this hunt to intercept that progression during the critical identity-harvesting window before the adversary reaches the encryption phase.

How the hunt flows

The first step identifies every host with VPN-related sign-in activity over the last 14 days. This scoping provides a list of potential beachheads and administrative workstations to focus the subsequent, more intensive queries. The second phase runs two queries in parallel. One query looks for authentication events where multi-factor authentication is absent or bypassed. The other query searches for PowerShell or shell script blocks containing offensive keywords like 'kerberoast' or 'minidump'. An analyst then triages these findings to link unhardened access with credential theft attempts on the same host. The third phase pivots to detect the impact. It checks for rare DNS lookups to exfiltration domains such as mega.nz or rclone.org. Simultaneously, it searches for process commands that delete volume shadow copies, which signifies the final stage before encryption. The final triage synthesizes the entire chain. If an analyst confirms a high-confidence match between the VPN entry and the subsequent impact commands, they authorize immediate host isolation and a double reset of the krbtgt account to revoke all active tickets.

What this hunt cannot see

This hunt relies on VPN provider logs appearing in the authentication surface. If the provider does not report MFA status or if logs are not ingested, the initial entry point remains hidden. Additionally, the script-based detection requires PowerShell Script Block Logging (Event 4104) to be active. If an adversary uses native binaries or obfuscated code without script logging, the harvesting phase stays invisible.

Steps

  1. Query, scoping

    Identify hosts with VPN activity

    Scope the estate to hosts acting as VPN gateways or beachheads based on authentication logs.

    sql, reads hb_auth_signin
    SELECT DISTINCT device_hostnameFROM hb_auth_signinWHERE (LOWER(event_type) LIKE '%vpn%' OR LOWER(auth_protocol) LIKE '%vpn%')  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. A list of hostnames with VPN-related sign-in events. Silence suggests no VPN telemetry is being ingested or no VPN activity occurred.

  2. Query, baseline

    VPN sign-ins without MFA

    Find VPN sessions where MFA was absent or bypassed, identifying potential beachhead accounts.

    sql, reads hb_auth_signin
    SELECT device_hostname, actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, mfa, event_type, timeFROM hb_auth_signinWHERE (mfa IS NULL OR LOWER(mfa) = 'false' OR mfa = '0')  AND (LOWER(event_type) LIKE '%vpn%' OR LOWER(auth_protocol) LIKE '%vpn%')  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. A list of users and source IPs using single-factor VPN access. Silence suggests MFA is enforced or the provider does not report it.

  3. Query, detection candidate

    Credential harvesting script blocks

    Detect Kerberoasting or LSASS memory dumping attempts within PowerShell or shell scripts.

    sql, reads hb_script_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, actor_user_name, script_name, script_content, timeFROM hb_script_activityWHERE (LOWER(script_content) LIKE '%kerberoast%' OR LOWER(script_content) LIKE '%get-domainspnticket%' OR LOWER(script_content) LIKE '%sekurlsa%' OR LOWER(script_content) LIKE '%minidump%lsass%')  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. Script blocks containing offensive identity keywords. Silence may indicate the use of native binaries or that script logging is absent.

  4. Agent triage

    Triage early access phase

    Weigh the VPN sign-in data against the credential harvesting evidence to confirm an account compromise.

  5. Query, baseline

    Rare cloud exfiltration DNS

    Find connections to exfiltration and tunneling domains that are rare across the fleet, suggesting targeted staging.

    sql, reads hb_dns_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, query_hostname, COUNT(*) AS lookups, MIN(time) AS first_seenFROM hb_dns_activityWHERE instr(',' || '{{exfil_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(query_hostname) || ',') > 0  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY device_hostname, query_hostname

    What a hit looks like. A small number of hosts resolving mega.nz or ngrok domains. Widespread traffic is likely legitimate software.

  6. Query, triage

    Volume shadow copy deletion

    Detect the use of native tools to destroy system recovery features, a precursor to widespread encryption.

    sql, reads hb_process_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, process_cmd_line, timeFROM hb_process_activityWHERE (LOWER(process_cmd_line) LIKE '%vssadmin%delete%shadows%' OR LOWER(process_cmd_line) LIKE '%wmic%shadowcopy%delete%')  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. Successful deletion commands. Silence is strong evidence the impact phase has not reached these specific hosts.

  7. Agent triage

    Triage full intrusion chain

    Synthesize early access findings with later impact evidence to confirm the full ransomware lifecycle.

  8. Decision

    Evaluate threat verdict

    Authorize immediate containment if the agent confirms a high-confidence ransomware scenario.

  9. Response action

    Isolate hosts and reset identities

    Immediately sever the attacker's network connection and revoke credentials.

  10. Analyst task

    Audit recovery safety

    Verify that backup infrastructure remains intact before promising a recovery time.

  11. Analyst task

    Incident wrap-up

    Record the findings and identify the exact window where the attacker moved from access to impact.

# VPN Entry and Identity Harvest

The adversary enters via a VPN that lacks multi-factor authentication, then harvests credentials to move laterally. This hunt identifies the unhardened sign-ins and the subsequent identity-focused script blocks. It then pivots to find evidence of outbound data transfer and the destruction of system backups. By correlating early-stage access with later-stage destructive behavior, the hunt provides the evidence required for an analyst to authorize isolation and identity resets before the encryption phase completes across the estate.
