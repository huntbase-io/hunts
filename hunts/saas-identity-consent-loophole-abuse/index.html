SaaS Identity and Consent Loophole Abuse

Medium severity TLP:CLEAR Part 1 of 2 Research by Elastic Security Labs Published 5 Nov 2026

Hypothesis

An adversary has bypassed MFA by stealing a session cookie via AiTM phishing or an infostealer, then logged into a cloud tenant and granted permissions to a malicious or rare application to ensure persistent access.

Brief

Why this hunt

Recent reporting from Elastic Security Labs in their When trust becomes the payload: Inside the threats, etc quarterly report highlights a shift in how adversaries maintain access to cloud environments. Rather than relying on simple credential theft, attackers use Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM) phishing or infostealers to hijack active session cookies. Once inside, they abuse the "consent loophole" by granting permissions to malicious or rare OAuth applications. This provides persistent access that survives password resets and MFA changes.

The Hunt Flow

The first phase examines the hb_auth_signin surface to identify successful logins marked as high-risk or originating from known anonymizing proxies. These leads represent the most likely entry points for session theft kits. We look for successful authentications that the provider's risk engine has already flagged, which often occurs when a stolen session is reused from a new, suspicious IP.

Once we have a set of suspect users, the hunt pivots into two parallel enrichment steps. The first step gathers detailed session attributes for these users, including user agents and device trust status, to establish a baseline of their normal activity. The second step shifts to the hb_cloud_api_activity surface to stack-count OAuth application consents. We specifically look for applications that have been granted permissions by five or fewer users across the entire fleet.

The final triage phase correlates these two data sets. We look for a specific pattern: a proxy-based or high-risk login followed quickly by a rare application consent or role assignment. Research indicates this often happens within an eight-minute window. An analyst or agent reviews these correlations to determine if the application is a legitimate internal tool or a malicious persistence mechanism.

Blind Spots

Log retention is the primary constraint. If an adversary granted consent to a malicious app 30 days ago but the hunt lookback is set to 14 days, the persistence mechanism remains invisible. Additionally, some SaaS providers do not explicitly report whether a session was established via a stolen cookie versus a fresh MFA challenge, which can lead to ambiguity during triage. We recommend extending Entra or Office 365 audit log retention to 90 days to mitigate this.

    What a hit looks like. Rows indicating successful logins despite high risk states or proxy origin. Silence indicates no obvious AiTM-style session usage was detected by the provider's risk engine.

  2. Query, enrichment

    Detail session attributes for the suspect users

    Collect detailed attributes for the sign-ins of the users identified in the lead, focusing on device trust and user agents.

    sql, reads hb_auth_signin
    SELECT time, actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, src_location_country, user_agent, client_app, auth_protocol, auth_requirement, device_trust_type, status_detailFROM hb_auth_signinWHERE status_id = 1  AND ('{{scope_users}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_users}}' || ',', ',' || actor_user_name || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. A history of sign-ins for the target users, allowing for comparison of location and device trust between normal and suspicious logins.

  3. Query, baseline

    Stack-count rare OAuth application consents

    Identify OAuth application consents or app role assignments for the targeted users that are rare across the fleet, highlighting potential malicious apps used for the consent loophole.

    sql, reads hb_cloud_api_activity
    SELECT resource_name AS app_name, api_operation, COUNT(DISTINCT actor_user_name) AS distinct_users, MIN(time) AS first_consent_time, MAX(time) AS last_consent_timeFROM hb_cloud_api_activityWHERE (LOWER(api_operation) LIKE '%consent%' OR LOWER(api_operation) LIKE '%add app role assignment%' OR LOWER(api_operation) LIKE '%update application%')  AND provider IN ('azure_ad', 'm365')  AND ('{{scope_users}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_users}}' || ',', ',' || actor_user_name || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY resource_name, api_operationHAVING distinct_users <= 5

    What a hit looks like. Applications that have been granted consent by only a few users, specifically those in our scope. If a user from the lead query is in this list, it strongly suggests identity abuse.

  4. Agent triage

    Analyze identity and consent patterns

    The agent determines whether the sign-ins and rare app consents constitute a session theft and persistence attempt.

  5. Decision

    Route based on identity threat verdict

    Initiate containment for malicious activity or refer suspicious cases to an analyst.

  6. Response action

    Revoke active sessions and tokens

    Stop active adversary access by revoking all refresh tokens and active sessions for the compromised account.

  7. Analyst task

    Verify remediation and check for persistence

    Ensure the adversary has no other persistence mechanisms like new administrative accounts or hidden enterprise applications.

  8. Analyst task

    Close out hunt

    Document findings and update any detection tuning based on the results.

# SaaS Identity and Consent Loophole Abuse

This hunt examines the identity and cloud control plane for signs of the consent loophole and AiTM phishing observed in the Nov 2026 report. It identifies successful logins originating from anonymizing proxies or carrying high-risk states, then corroborates these by looking for rare OAuth application consent events associated with those same users. The hunt focuses on the critical 8-minute window where session theft transitions to persistent cloud access through first-party app abuse.
