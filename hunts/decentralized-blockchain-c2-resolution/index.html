Decentralized Blockchain C2 Resolution

High severity TLP:CLEAR Part 1 of 2 Research by Cisco Talos Published 19 Nov 2026

Hypothesis

An adversary is using public blockchain ledgers as dead-drop resolvers for C2 configuration, identified by outbound RPC calls to node providers followed by rare network beaconing to resolved endpoints.

Brief

Resilience through Decentralization

Adversaries increasingly use public blockchains to host command-and-control (C2) configuration. As detailed in the Talos Intelligence article, The challenge of defending against bulletproof dead drops on public blockchains, attackers store C2 IP addresses or domains within immutable ledger transactions. Malware then queries these ledgers via legitimate RPC (Remote Procedure Call) providers to find its next hop. This technique makes traditional takedowns and sinkholing nearly impossible because the resolution mechanism relies on a globally distributed, permanent database.

How the Hunt Flows

The hunt begins by scoping hosts that resolve known blockchain RPC provider domains. The first query searches DNS logs for lookups to infrastructure like infura.io or solana.com. While developers and research teams frequently use these services, the goal here is to identify candidate systems that act as starting points for deeper inspection.

Next, the hunt narrows the focus to active communication. It examines HTTP POST activity from the candidate hosts to the identified RPC providers. Because retrieving data from a blockchain requires sending a JSON-RPC payload to an endpoint, these POST requests represent the actual data retrieval event. The hunt looks for unusual User-Agents or process names that do not match standard browser or developer tools.

Finally, the hunt correlates the RPC traffic with outbound network activity. It identifies rare connections to external IP addresses that follow the blockchain resolution event. The logic prioritizes connections to non-standard ports (excluding 80, 443, and 53). A high-confidence lead emerges when the same process that performed the RPC lookup, or a related child process, initiates a new connection to an external endpoint that has low prevalence across the rest of the environment.

Blind Spots

This hunt faces limitations due to encryption. Most RPC providers use HTTPS, which masks the specific JSON-RPC method call and the resulting payload from network-level inspection. Unless the environment employs TLS decryption, the hunt cannot verify if the malware is calling a specific transaction or simply checking the status of the node. Furthermore, if the malware uses a private or internal RPC gateway not included in the provider list, the initial scoping query will not flag the activity.

Steps

  1. Query, scoping

    DNS lookups to blockchain RPC nodes

    Identify hosts and processes resolving known blockchain RPC provider domains as a lead for potential dead-drop resolution.

    sql, reads hb_dns_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, process_name, pid, query_hostname, COUNT(*) AS lookup_count, MIN(time) AS first_lookupFROM hb_dns_activityWHERE (instr(',' || '{{rpc_providers}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(query_hostname) || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY device_hostname, process_name, pid, query_hostname

    What a hit looks like. Hosts and specific processes resolving blockchain domains. Legitimate developer activity will show high volume across multiple distinct providers; malicious resolution is typically low volume and tied to unusual processes.

  2. Query, enrichment

    HTTP POST activity to RPC endpoints

    Focus on the HTTP POST requests that carry JSON-RPC payloads, which are required for querying specific blockchain data like transactions or logs.

    sql, reads hb_http_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, url_hostname, url_path, http_method, user_agent, timeFROM hb_http_activityWHERE (instr(',' || '{{rpc_providers}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(url_hostname) || ',') > 0)  AND http_method = 'POST'  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. POST requests to RPC providers from the scoping hosts. Analysts should look for unusual User-Agents or paths that do not match standard browser or developer tool patterns.

  3. Query, baseline

    Rare outbound connections from RPC hosts

    Identify non-standard outbound connections occurring on hosts that have also performed blockchain RPC lookups, prioritizing non-standard ports.

    sql, reads hb_network_connection
    SELECT device_hostname, dst_endpoint_ip, dst_endpoint_port, process_name, COUNT(DISTINCT device_hostname) AS host_count, MIN(time) AS first_seenFROM hb_network_connectionWHERE direction = 'outbound'  AND disposition = 'Allowed'  AND (dst_endpoint_port NOT IN (80, 443, 53))  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY dst_endpoint_ip, dst_endpoint_port, process_nameHAVING host_count <= 3ORDER BY host_count ASC

    What a hit looks like. A connection to a rare external IP address on a non-standard port. Correlation is strongest when this connection follows an RPC lookup by the same host.

  4. Agent triage

    Evaluate blockchain-to-C2 sequence

    Analyze the temporal and behavioural relationship between blockchain interaction and rare network egress.

  5. Decision

    Route on triage verdict

    Direct the hunt based on the agent's correlation of blockchain resolution and beaconing behaviour.

  6. Response action

    Isolate host and capture memory

    Neutralize the threat and preserve the in-memory artifacts characteristic of Overlord RAT and similar blockchain-backed malware.

  7. Analyst task

    Analyst forensic review

    Verify the agent's findings and investigate the origin of the malicious process.

  8. Analyst task

    Hunt closure and tuning

    Record findings and update exclusions to reduce false positives for legitimate developer activity.

# Decentralized Blockchain C2 Resolution

This hunt identifies attempts to resolve command-and-control infrastructure using Web3 nodes. It looks for DNS and HTTP traffic directed at public blockchain RPC providers that correlate with rare outbound network connections. By abusing immutable ledgers like Solana and Ethereum, attackers bypass traditional domain sinkholing. The hunt baselines typical developer RPC usage and highlights hosts that reach out to the blockchain and then initiate unique outbound sessions, often targeting non-standard ports within a narrow temporal window.

<!-- Isolate host and capture memory -->
```action target=endpoint
~~~yaml

```

## analyst-review
<!-- Analyst forensic review -->
```manual target=analyst
Review the process tree for the processes identified in the RPC lookups and outbound connections. Inspect any available HTTP payload data for Base64 or hex-encoded C2 strings. Validate if the activity matches the Overlord RAT's Solana-based dead-drop pattern.
```
→ close-out

## close-out
<!-- Hunt closure and tuning -->
```manual target=analyst
Document all confirmed malicious hosts and the resolved C2 IP addresses. If legitimate developer workstations were flagged, record their hostnames and typical RPC patterns for inclusion in the scope_hosts exclusion list for future runs.
```
→ end
