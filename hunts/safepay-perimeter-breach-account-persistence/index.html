SafePay Ransomware: Perimeter Breach and Account Persistence

An adversary has exploited unpatched edge vulnerabilities or use VPN-based brute force to gain initial access, subsequently creating or elevating accounts to secure administrative persistence.

Brief

Why now

CISA recently published AA26-293A: #StopRansomware: SafePay Ransomware, detailing the tactics of a group targeting critical infrastructure. SafePay actors rely on common vulnerabilities in edge devices and backup solutions to enter a network. They often maintain access by creating new administrative accounts, a move that provides long-term persistence even if their initial entry point is patched. This hunt targets the gap between the perimeter breach and the deployment of ransomware.

How the hunt flows

The hunt begins by scoping the environment for vulnerable infrastructure. The first query identifies hosts reporting unpatched CVEs associated with SafePay, such as CVE-2024-40766 or CVE-2023-27532. This step defines the primary surface where an intrusion is most likely to originate.

Next, the hunt moves into a parallel analysis phase. One query examines authentication logs for sign-ins originating from Mullvad VPN IPs or known anonymizing proxies. This identifies potential brute force or unauthorized access attempts against the vulnerable infrastructure found in the scoping phase.

Simultaneously, the hunt monitors for persistence markers. Two queries look for account management events: the creation of new user accounts and the addition of users to privileged groups (e.g., Domain Admins or local Administrators). The hunt focuses on changes made by unusual actors or service accounts that do not follow standard provisioning patterns.

In the final phase, an agent or analyst weighs the evidence. A match on a vulnerable host combined with a proxy-based sign-in and a subsequent administrative group change provides a high-fidelity indicator of a breach. This correlation differentiates the hunt from a simple detection rule, which might trigger on a single legitimate admin change but ignore the preceding perimeter signals.

Blind spots

This hunt depends on the availability of authentication logs. If a VPN appliance or edge device does not forward sign-in events to a central log source, the hunt cannot see the initial access attempts. Additionally, if the adversary uses a private proxy or a new Mullvad exit node not included in current IP lists, the authentication queries may return false negatives. Analysts should verify that edge telemetry is actively flowing before relying on these results.

    What a hit looks like. A list of devices and their unpatched CVEs. A match indicates an exposed surface area matching SafePay's known targeting.

  2. VPN and proxy authentication patterns

    Query · detection candidate

    Find sign-in attempts or successful logons originating from Mullvad VPN or anonymizing proxies.

    reads hb_auth_signinsql
    SELECT src_endpoint_ip, actor_user_name, status, COUNT(*) AS attempts, MIN(time) AS first_attempt, MAX(time) AS last_attempt FROM hb_auth_signin WHERE (is_proxy = 'true' OR instr(',' || '{{mullvad_ips}}' || ',', ',' || src_endpoint_ip || ',') > 0) AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY src_endpoint_ip, actor_user_name, status

    What a hit looks like. A successful logon from a proxy or Mullvad IP is a high-fidelity indicator of account compromise or external unauthorized access.

  3. New administrators and group additions

    Query · triage

    Detect the addition of accounts to privileged groups, a key SafePay persistence mechanism.

    reads hb_group_managementsql
    SELECT device_hostname, actor_user_name, group_name, user_name, activity_name, time FROM hb_group_management WHERE (LOWER(group_name) LIKE '%admin%' OR group_uid = 'S-1-5-32-544') AND activity_id = 3 AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. A row identifying an account added to an administrative group. If the actor_user_name is a service account or an unusual user, it is highly suspicious.

  4. Suspicious account creation

    Query · triage

    Detect the creation of new user accounts which SafePay actors use to establish control.

    reads hb_account_changesql
    SELECT device_hostname, user_name, actor_user_name, activity_name, time FROM hb_account_change WHERE activity_id = 1 AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. The creation of a new account not associated with documented provisioning activity.

  5. Weigh perimeter and account evidence

    Agent triage

    Evaluate whether the identified vulnerabilities, sign-in anomalies, and account changes suggest an active SafePay intrusion.

  6. Route on verdict

# SafePay Ransomware: Perimeter Breach and Account Persistence

SafePay ransomware actors frequently use vulnerabilities in edge devices, such as Cisco ASA/FTD or Veeam components, or perform brute-force attacks from known VPN providers like Mullvad to gain a foothold. Once inside, they prioritize the creation of new administrative accounts or the addition of users to local/domain admin groups to ensure long-term control before moving laterally. This hunt identifies vulnerable infrastructure, correlates access attempts from high-risk IP addresses, and monitors for suspicious account management activities that signify a beachhead establishment.
