Optimizely Injected Script: Visitor, Session and Site-Owner Exposure

High severity TLP:CLEAR Research by BleepingComputer Published 18 Nov 2026

Hypothesis

Staff browsers that visited a site carrying the tampered Optimizely snippet on 16 Nov 2026 loaded the attacker's script, and some sent card details, readable cookies or iPhone traffic to the attacker; separately, the organisation's own Optimizely project may have served the script to its customers.

Brief

Why This Hunt Matters

On 16 November 2026 an attacker added JavaScript to Optimizely Web Experimentation projects belonging to 178 organisations, as BleepingComputer reported. Scott Helme at Report URI found the code in 188 of the 1,611 Optimizely snippets he checked. Because the code sat in each project's own JavaScript, sites such as BBC, CNN, Zoom, GitLab and Salesforce served it from Optimizely's CDN, under Optimizely's certificate, from about 04:21 UTC until clean-up finished at 23:11 UTC.

The script, track.js from 1688360.cc, did four things. It swapped cryptocurrency addresses on the page. From 13:43 UTC it skimmed card numbers, expiry dates and security codes to /card.php. After a swap it sent the page URL, readable cookies and a screenshot to /hit.php. On iPhones and iPads it loaded the Coruna and DarkSword exploit kits.

The domain is now dead, but that does not settle anything. Anyone on your staff who typed a card, held a wallet page open or was signed in on an affected page during those hours may already have lost data. If your organisation runs Optimizely itself, your own customers may have been the victims.

How the Hunt Flows

The hunt opens with DNS. It lists every managed device that looked up 1688360.cc, the earlier injector webshell.lol, or one of the .icu hosts used for the iOS stage. This is the widest list of exposed browsers, and its hosts feed the later steps.

Six queries then run side by side. Proxy logs show who requested the payload host, and the referrer names the site they were on. A second proxy query looks for the data leaving: POSTs to /card.php mean a card was submitted, and POSTs to /hit.php mean cookies and a screenshot were sent. A third picks out iPhones and iPads that reached the exploit hosts. A connection query on the attacker's last known IP addresses catches browsers that used DNS-over-HTTPS and bypassed the proxy.

Two steps do not depend on the published hosts at all. One finds hosts first seen during the exposure window that browsers fetched scripts or .php paths from while on a different site, ranked by how many unrelated sites referred to them and how often an Optimizely snippet was fetched just before. The other stack-counts domains that only the exposed devices looked up for the first time. Both are noisy, because new ad and analytics hosts appear every day, but a rotated host would show up there.

An agent combines this into a table of who loaded the script, who lost a card, who lost cookies and which phones were reached. For those users, the hunt checks sign-ins from addresses they had not used before. A second agent decides whether a session was stolen or reused. Page JavaScript cannot read HttpOnly cookies, so the risk depends on how each site set its cookies.

If theft is likely, the hunt proposes revoking the user's sessions and tokens, gated by approval. It does not isolate laptops: the code ran inside a web page and left nothing on the device. Manual steps follow for card reissue, iPhone version checks, the organisation's own Optimizely projects and crypto transfers to the attacker's wallets.

What the Hunt Cannot See

Without TLS inspection the proxy logs the host but not the path or method, so a script load cannot be told apart from card theft. Phones on cellular data never reach corporate logs, and those are the devices the exploit kits targeted. Browsers using DNS-over-HTTPS skip the DNS logs. One loader variant ran an embedded copy of the payload when track.js could not load, so no script fetch appears. Cookie replay into a SaaS app does not create an identity-provider sign-in, so the sign-in step can be silent while a session is abused. A swapped crypto address leaves no network trace of its own. Changes to your own Optimizely project are only visible in Optimizely. Finally, the logs must still reach back to 9 November.

Steps

  1. Query, scoping

    Hosts that resolved the attacker domains

    Find managed devices that looked up the payload host or the iOS stage hosts. DNS sees the lookup even when the proxy cannot see the URL, so this is the widest list of browsers that ran the injected code.

    sql, reads hb_dns_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, actor_user_name, process_name, LOWER(query_hostname) AS domain, COUNT(*) AS lookups, MIN(time) AS first_seen, MAX(time) AS last_seenFROM hb_dns_activityWHERE (instr(',' || '{{payload_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(query_hostname) || ',') > 0 OR instr(',' || '{{ios_stage_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(query_hostname) || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY device_hostname, actor_user_name, process_name, LOWER(query_hostname)ORDER BY first_seen

    What a hit looks like. Devices and users whose browser looked up 1688360.cc, webshell.lol or an iOS stage host, with first and last lookup. Paste the hosts into scope_hosts. Silence does not clear the estate: browsers using DNS-over-HTTPS bypass these logs.

  2. Query, triage

    Proxy requests to the attacker hosts

    List every proxied request to the payload host, with the referring page. The referrer names the site the user was on when the injected snippet ran, and the user agent shows the browser and device.

    sql, reads hb_http_activity
    SELECT src_endpoint_ip, actor_user_name, LOWER(url_hostname) AS host, url_path, http_method, referrer, user_agent, status_code, COUNT(*) AS requests, MIN(time) AS first_seen, MAX(time) AS last_seenFROM hb_http_activityWHERE instr(',' || '{{payload_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(url_hostname) || ',') > 0  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY src_endpoint_ip, actor_user_name, LOWER(url_hostname), url_path, http_method, referrer, user_agent, status_codeORDER BY first_seen

    What a hit looks like. GET /track.js (or /get.js on webshell.lol) with a referrer on a site that used Optimizely, from a named user or client IP. Here device_hostname is the proxy, so identify the person by actor_user_name or src_endpoint_ip. Without TLS inspection only the host is logged, not the path.

  3. Query, triage

    Card and cookie data sent to the payload host

    Find requests that carried stolen data out: /card.php receives card number, expiry and CVV after a card form is submitted; /hit.php receives the page URL, readable cookies and a screenshot after a wallet address is swapped. Any POST to the payload host is included, because the inline fallback variant sends data without first fetching track.js.

    sql, reads hb_http_activity
    SELECT src_endpoint_ip, actor_user_name, LOWER(url_hostname) AS host, url_path, http_method, referrer, user_agent, request_body_len, status_code, timeFROM hb_http_activityWHERE instr(',' || '{{payload_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(url_hostname) || ',') > 0  AND (instr(',' || '{{exfil_paths}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(url_path) || ',') > 0 OR UPPER(http_method) = 'POST' OR activity_id = 6)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')ORDER BY time

    What a hit looks like. A POST to /card.php means that user submitted a card on an affected page; a POST to /hit.php means the clipper fired on a wallet or payment page and sent the page's readable cookies. The referrer is the affected page. Needs TLS inspection to see the path and method.

  4. Query, triage

    iPhone and iPad traffic to the exploit hosts

    Find iPhones and iPads that reached the iOS stage hosts, or that loaded the payload. track.js only loaded the exploit chain for iPhone and iPad user agents.

    sql, reads hb_http_activity
    SELECT src_endpoint_ip, actor_user_name, LOWER(url_hostname) AS host, url_path, referrer, user_agent, COUNT(*) AS requests, MIN(time) AS first_seen, MAX(time) AS last_seenFROM hb_http_activityWHERE (instr(',' || '{{ios_stage_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(url_hostname) || ',') > 0 OR (instr(',' || '{{payload_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(url_hostname) || ',') > 0 AND (LOWER(user_agent) LIKE '%iphone%' OR LOWER(user_agent) LIKE '%ipad%' OR LOWER(user_agent) LIKE '%ipod%')))  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY src_endpoint_ip, actor_user_name, LOWER(url_hostname), url_path, referrer, user_agentORDER BY first_seen

    What a hit looks like. boot.js from a .icu host, or requests under /A8/, /B9/, /D11/, /D12/, /C10/ or /JIUJIU888/, from a corporate-Wi-Fi or VPN iPhone or iPad. The iOS version in the user agent decides the follow-up. iPadOS Safari often sends a desktop Mac user agent, so a .icu hit from a 'Mac' still counts.

  5. Query, triage

    Connections to the attacker addresses

    Catch browsers whose lookups never reached the DNS logs (DNS-over-HTTPS) and whose traffic did not pass the proxy, by the last known IP addresses.

    sql, reads hb_network_connection
    SELECT device_hostname, user_name, process_name, dst_endpoint_ip, dst_endpoint_port, COUNT(*) AS connections, MIN(time) AS first_seen, MAX(time) AS last_seenFROM hb_network_connectionWHERE instr(',' || '{{attacker_ips}}' || ',', ',' || dst_endpoint_ip || ',') > 0  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY device_hostname, user_name, process_name, dst_endpoint_ip, dst_endpoint_portORDER BY first_seen

    What a hit looks like. A browser process connecting to one of the addresses on 443 inside the exposure window. These are Google Cloud addresses that can be reassigned, so a match outside 9 to 17 Nov 2026 is weak on its own.

  6. Query, baseline

    New cross-site script and .php hosts

    Behaviour that survives a change of host: during the exposure window, find hosts never seen in the baseline that browsers fetched script or .php paths from while on a different site. Rank them by how many unrelated sites referred to them and by how often the same client fetched an Optimizely snippet just before. One new host referred to by many unrelated sites is the shape of an injected third-party script.

    sql, reads hb_http_activity
    SELECT LOWER(h.url_hostname) AS host, COUNT(DISTINCT h.referrer) AS referring_pages, COUNT(DISTINCT h.src_endpoint_ip) AS clients, COUNT(DISTINCT h.actor_user_name) AS users, SUM(CASE WHEN UPPER(h.http_method) = 'POST' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS posts, SUM(CASE WHEN EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM hb_http_activity o WHERE o.src_endpoint_ip = h.src_endpoint_ip AND instr(',' || '{{optimizely_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(o.url_hostname) || ',') > 0 AND julianday(o.time) BETWEEN julianday(h.time) - {{pivot_minutes}} / 1440.0 AND julianday(h.time)) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS after_optimizely, MIN(h.time) AS first_seen, MAX(h.time) AS last_seenFROM hb_http_activity hWHERE h.time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')  AND julianday(h.time) BETWEEN julianday('{{exposure_start}}') AND julianday('{{exposure_end}}')  AND (LOWER(h.url_path) LIKE '%.js' OR LOWER(h.url_path) LIKE '%.php')  AND h.referrer IS NOT NULL  AND h.referrer != ''  AND instr(LOWER(h.referrer), LOWER(h.url_hostname)) = 0  AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM hb_http_activity b WHERE LOWER(b.url_hostname) = LOWER(h.url_hostname) AND b.time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') AND julianday(b.time) < julianday('{{exposure_start}}'))GROUP BY LOWER(h.url_hostname)ORDER BY referring_pages DESC, after_optimizely DESC, posts DESCLIMIT 200

    What a hit looks like. 1688360.cc near the top if the proxy saw it. Any other first-seen host with many unrelated referring pages, POSTs to .php, and fetches soon after an Optimizely snippet is a candidate rotated host. Expect noise from new ad, analytics and CDN hosts; the referring-pages count and after_optimizely are what separate them. Snippets served from a first-party host or a consent manager do not show as Optimizely fetches.

  7. Query, baseline

    First-seen domains on exposed hosts

    On the hosts from the scoping step, stack-count the domains first looked up during the exposure window across the fleet. Rare domains looked up only by exposed hosts may be a rotated payload or exfiltration host the published list misses.

    sql, reads hb_dns_activity
    SELECT LOWER(d.query_hostname) AS domain, COUNT(DISTINCT d.device_hostname) AS devices, GROUP_CONCAT(DISTINCT d.process_name) AS processes, MIN(d.time) AS first_seen, MAX(d.time) AS last_seenFROM hb_dns_activity dWHERE ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || d.device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND d.time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')  AND julianday(d.time) BETWEEN julianday('{{exposure_start}}') AND julianday('{{exposure_end}}')  AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM hb_dns_activity b WHERE LOWER(b.query_hostname) = LOWER(d.query_hostname) AND b.time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') AND julianday(b.time) < julianday('{{exposure_start}}'))GROUP BY LOWER(d.query_hostname)HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT d.device_hostname) < 20ORDER BY devices DESCLIMIT 200

    What a hit looks like. Newly seen domains shared by several exposed hosts, especially random-looking .cc or .icu names like the published ones. Most rows will be ordinary new domains; read it with the referrers from the proxy steps.

  8. Agent triage

    Weigh exposure per user and device

    Turn the six evidence sets into a per-user, per-device exposure picture.

  9. Query, enrichment

    Sign-ins from new addresses after exposure

    For the exposed users, find successful sign-ins from source addresses the user had not used before the exposure window. Stolen cookies are replayed rather than signed in with, so this catches only the cases where the cookie led to a new sign-in or token refresh the identity provider logged.

    sql, reads hb_auth_signin
    SELECT s.actor_user_name, s.provider, s.app_id, s.dst_endpoint_name, s.client_app, s.src_endpoint_ip, s.src_location_country, s.user_agent, COUNT(*) AS sign_ins, MIN(s.time) AS first_seen, MAX(s.time) AS last_seenFROM hb_auth_signin sWHERE s.time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')  AND julianday(s.time) >= julianday('{{exposure_start}}')  AND s.status_id = 1  AND s.src_endpoint_ip IS NOT NULL  AND ('{{scope_users}}' = '' OR instr(',' || LOWER('{{scope_users}}') || ',', ',' || LOWER(s.actor_user_name) || ',') > 0)  AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM hb_auth_signin b WHERE LOWER(b.actor_user_name) = LOWER(s.actor_user_name) AND b.src_endpoint_ip = s.src_endpoint_ip AND b.time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') AND julianday(b.time) < julianday('{{exposure_start}}'))GROUP BY s.actor_user_name, s.provider, s.app_id, s.dst_endpoint_name, s.client_app, s.src_endpoint_ip, s.src_location_country, s.user_agentORDER BY first_seen

    What a hit looks like. A successful sign-in for an exposed user from a new address or country after the exposure, especially to Salesforce, GitLab, Zoom, Smartsheet or Bitly, whose public pages carried the snippet. Silence is weak: cookie replay into the SaaS app itself appears in that app's audit log, not here.

  10. Agent triage

    Assess session theft and reuse

    Decide whether any exposed user's session was stolen or reused.

  11. Decision

    Route on session verdict

    Revoke sessions where theft is likely; otherwise review exposure and the owner-side checks.

  12. Response action

    Revoke sessions of affected users

    End sessions that may have been stolen from an injected page.

  13. Analyst task

    Follow up cards and iPhones

    Handle what the queries can only point at: payment cards and mobile devices.

  14. Analyst task

    Check your own Optimizely projects

    If the organisation runs Optimizely Web Experimentation, its own visitors may have been exposed.

  15. Analyst task

    Check crypto transfers against attacker wallets

    Address swapping happens inside the page and leaves no network trace of its own.

Blind spots

  • Needs TLS inspection on the forward proxy (or a secure web gateway that logs full URLs and methods). Without decryption the proxy logs the host but not the path or method, so card and cookie theft cannot be told apart from a script load and every visitor must be treated as possibly skimmed. It would answer: Did the browser fetch /track.js or POST to /card.php and /hit.php, or only connect to the host?
  • Needs MDM inventory with iOS versions and mobile threat defence on corporate and BYOD iPhones and iPads. Phones on cellular data or home Wi-Fi never touch the proxy or DNS logs, and the exploit kits targeted exactly those devices. It would answer: Which iPhones and iPads visited an affected site, on what iOS version, and off the corporate network?
  • Needs DNS-over-HTTPS disabled by browser policy, or DoH resolver traffic logged. Lookups made over DoH do not reach hb_dns_activity, so the scoping step under-counts exposed hosts. It would answer: Did browsers resolve 1688360.cc through a built-in DoH resolver?
  • Needs At least 30 days of proxy and DNS log retention, reaching back to 9 Nov 2026. Short retention removes the exposure window entirely and silence then means nothing. It would answer: Do the logs still cover 9 to 17 Nov 2026?
  • Needs Client-side script monitoring or CSP reporting on the visited sites. One loader variant ran an embedded copy of the payload with eval when track.js could not load, so no script fetch is logged; only its later POSTs to the payload host can show, and a network block on that host stops those too. (The atob() variant still fetches track.js; it only defeats string searches of the snippet.) It would answer: Did a browser run the inline copy of the payload that needs no track.js fetch?
  • Needs Audit logs of the affected SaaS apps (session and IP history) and the HttpOnly flags of their session cookies. Replaying a cookie does not create an identity-provider sign-in, so hb_auth_signin can be silent while a session is abused. HttpOnly cookies were not readable by the script, so the real risk depends on each site's cookie flags. It would answer: Was a cookie sent to /hit.php replayed against the app it came from?
  • Needs Optimizely project change history, API token list and CSP violation reports for the organisation's own sites. None of this is in hb_ telemetry; if the organisation's snippet carried the payload, its customers were the victims and only the manual review finds it. It would answer: Was the organisation's own Optimizely project changed on 16 Nov 2026?
  • Needs Transfer records from exchanges and wallets used by the organisation. The swap happens in the page and clipboard; only /hit.php shows it fired, and only with TLS inspection. It would answer: Did anyone send cryptocurrency to a swapped address?

# Optimizely Injected Script: Visitor, Session and Site-Owner Exposure

On 16 Nov 2026 malicious JavaScript was added to Optimizely Web Experimentation projects of 178 organisations, so sites such as BBC, CNN, Zoom, GitLab and Salesforce served it to their visitors from Optimizely's own CDN for most of a day. This hunt answers three questions. Which of our people loaded it, and what did it take from them: card details, cookies, or an iPhone exploit attempt? Was any stolen session reused? And, if we run Optimizely ourselves, did our own site serve it? It starts from DNS, proxy and connection logs for the published hosts, adds two behaviour steps that look for new cross-site script hosts so a rotated host is not missed, checks sign-ins for the exposed users, and ends with manual checks for cards, iPhones, our own Optimizely projects and crypto transfers.

## resolve-attacker-hosts
<!-- Hosts that resolved the attacker domains -->
Find managed devices that looked up the payload host or the iOS stage hosts. DNS sees the lookup even when the proxy cannot see the URL, so this is the widest list of browsers that ran the injected code.

```sqlite target=endpoint role=scoping params=(payload_domains=payload_domains, ios_stage_domains=ios_stage_domains, lookback_days=lookback_days)
~~~yaml
```

## new-script-hosts-after-optimizely
<!-- New cross-site script and .php hosts -->
Behaviour that survives a change of host: during the exposure window, find hosts never seen in the baseline that browsers fetched script or .php paths from while on a different site. Rank them by how many unrelated sites referred to them and by how often the same client fetched an Optimizely snippet just before. One new host referred to by many unrelated sites is the shape of an injected third-party script.

```sqlite target=web role=baseline params=(optimizely_domains=optimizely_domains, exposure_start=exposure_start, exposure_end=exposure_end, pivot_minutes=pivot_minutes, lookback_days=lookback_days)
~~~yaml
expected: 1688360.cc near the top if the proxy saw it. Any other first-seen host with
  many unrelated referring pages, POSTs to .php, and fetches soon after an Optimizely
  snippet is a candidate rotated host. Expect noise from new ad, analytics and CDN
  hosts; the referring-pages count and after_optimizely are what separate them. Snippets
  served from a first-party host or a consent manager do not show as Optimizely fetches.
reads:
- actor_user_name
- http_method
- referrer
- src_endpoint_ip
- time
- url_hostname
- url_path
silence: not_evidence_of_absence
source: hb_http_activity
verified: dry-run
verified_at: '2026-11-18'
~~~
SELECT LOWER(h.url_hostname) AS host, COUNT(DISTINCT h.referrer) AS referring_pages, COUNT(DISTINCT h.src_endpoint_ip) AS clients, COUNT(DISTINCT h.actor_user_name) AS users, SUM(CASE WHEN UPPER(h.http_method) = 'POST' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS posts, SUM(CASE WHEN EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM hb_http_activity o WHERE o.src_endpoint_ip = h.src_endpoint_ip AND instr(',' || '{{optimizely_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(o.url_hostname) || ',') > 0 AND julianday(o.time) BETWEEN julianday(h.time) - {{pivot_minutes}} / 1440.0 AND julianday(h.time)) THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS after_optimizely, MIN(h.time) AS first_seen, MAX(h.time) AS last_seen FROM hb_http_activity h WHERE h.time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') AND julianday(h.time) BETWEEN julianday('{{exposure_start}}') AND julianday('{{exposure_end}}') AND (LOWER(h.url_path) LIKE '%.js' OR LOWER(h.url_path) LIKE '%.php') AND h.referrer IS NOT NULL AND h.referrer != '' AND instr(LOWER(h.referrer), LOWER(h.url_hostname)) = 0 AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM hb_http_activity b WHERE LOWER(b.url_hostname) = LOWER(h.url_hostname) AND b.time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') AND julianday(b.time) < julianday('{{exposure_start}}')) GROUP BY LOWER(h.url_hostname) ORDER BY referring_pages DESC, after_optimizely DESC, posts DESC LIMIT 200
```

## exposed-user-follow-up
<!-- Follow up cards and iPhones -->
```manual target=analyst
Cards: for every user with a /card.php POST, and for anyone who paid by card on an affected site between 04:00 and 23:11 UTC on 16 Nov 2026, check the corporate or purchasing card statements, ask the issuer to reissue the card and watch for fraud. Tell users to do the same with personal cards. iPhones and iPads: for each device that reached an iOS stage host or loaded the payload, check the iOS version and Lockdown Mode status in MDM. In this campaign Coruna was served to iOS 13 to 17 (Google's analysis of the kit gives 13.0 to 17.2.1), DarkSword to iOS 18.x (Google: 18.4 to 18.7), and iOS 26 was only fingerprinted. The kit treats 18.7.3 and 26.3 as patched; one DarkSword bug stays open until 26.3. Devices on iOS 18.6 or earlier also loaded a hidden iframe and reloaded the page every 60 seconds. Update every device below 26.3 (or 18.7.3 where 26 is not available), review mobile threat defence alerts, and send any device with signs of compromise for forensic review with the vendor's tooling (for example MVT with the iVerify Coruna indicators). Lockdown Mode stops both kits. No source has shown a device was actually exploited in this campaign.
```
