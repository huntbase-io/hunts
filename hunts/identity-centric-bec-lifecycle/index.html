Identity-Centric Business Email Compromise

Medium severity TLP:CLEAR Research by Huntress Published 15 Oct 2026

Hypothesis

An adversary has hijacked an identity through an Adversary-in-the-Middle proxy or by exploiting a public-facing vulnerability, then manipulates mailbox rules to hide fraudulent communication and spreads malicious internal attachments.

Brief

Why Now

Business Email Compromise remains a primary driver of financial loss across organizations. While many teams focus on the initial phishing email, the actual impact happens after the identity is lost. The Huntress article Business Email Compromise (BEC) Attacks: How to Stop Them outlines how attackers move from proxy-based logins to internal lateral movement and financial redirection. This hunt implements that research as a structured playbook.

How the Hunt Flows

The hunt begins by identifying the attack surface. A scoping query finds internet-facing devices with high-severity vulnerabilities. These assets often serve as the initial point of entry for attackers seeking to gain a foothold in the network before moving to identity-based attacks.

In the second phase, the hunt gathers evidence of initial access. Two queries run in parallel: one identifies logins originating from known anonymizing proxies, and another monitors endpoint HTTP traffic for communication with known AiTM phishing domains. An automated agent then triages these signals to determine which users show a high likelihood of account takeover.

The third phase investigates persistence and internal spread. One query looks for new or modified M365 mailbox rules, specifically those that move mail to the RSS Feeds or Deleted Items folders to hide attacker activity. A second query monitors common email clients like Outlook for suspicious child processes, such as PowerShell or CMD, which indicate a malicious attachment has been executed.

A final synthesis step brings all findings together. An analyst confirms the verdict by correlating the rare proxy logins with subsequent mailbox manipulation or endpoint execution. This sequence differentiates a compromised account from an administrator performing routine maintenance.

What the Hunt Cannot See

This hunt has two primary blind spots. First, cloud audit latency in M365 means rule changes may not appear in the telemetry immediately after they occur. Attackers can manipulate rules and delete evidence within minutes, potentially escaping the hunt window. Second, without full TLS inspection, the HTTP traffic search only sees the destination hostname. If an attacker uses a legitimate compromised service for phishing, the specific malicious URI remains hidden.

    Identify exposed vulnerable assets

    Find internet-facing devices with high-severity vulnerabilities that could serve as initial access vectors.

    sql, reads hb_vulnerability_finding

    What a hit looks like. Hosts with unpatched critical vulnerabilities. Silence suggests the external surface is well-defended against known exploits.

  2. Query, baseline

    Stack-count proxy-based logins

    Find logins originating from anonymizing proxies to highlight rare actor behavior.

    sql, reads hb_auth_signin
    SELECT src_endpoint_ip, COUNT(DISTINCT actor_user_name) AS user_count, COUNT(*) AS login_attempts, MIN(time) AS first_seenFROM hb_auth_signinWHERE is_proxy = 'true'  AND status_id = 1  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY src_endpoint_ipHAVING user_count <= 2ORDER BY user_count ASC

    What a hit looks like. A list of proxy IPs used by only one or two accounts, suggesting targeted attacker access.

  3. Query, triage

    Hunt for AiTM proxy domains

    Identify endpoints communicating with known phishing or AiTM proxy domains.

    sql, reads hb_http_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, src_endpoint_ip, url_hostname, url_path, user_agent, timeFROM hb_http_activityWHERE instr(',' || '{{phishing_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(url_hostname) || ',') > 0  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)

    What a hit looks like. Hosts contacting malicious domains. Silence means these specific domains were not used in the lookback window.

  4. Agent triage

    Triage initial identity risk

    Assess whether the observed proxy logins and HTTP traffic together confirm a high-likelihood account takeover.

  5. Query, detection candidate

    Monitor for mailbox rule persistence

    Detect the creation of inbox rules used to hide attacker communication or redirect mail.

    sql, reads hb_cloud_api_activity
    SELECT cloud_account_uid, actor_user_name, api_operation, resource_name, status_detail, timeFROM hb_cloud_api_activityWHERE api_service_name = 'm365'  AND instr(',' || '{{mailbox_ops}}' || ',', ',' || api_operation || ',') > 0  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. Users creating rules to move mail to RSS Feeds or Deleted Items. Silence proves no such rules were created during the audit window.

  6. Query, triage

    Execution from email clients

    Identify suspicious child processes launched from common email clients.

    sql, reads hb_process_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, user_name, process_name, process_cmd_line, parent_process_name, timeFROM hb_process_activityWHERE instr(',' || '{{email_clients}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(parent_process_name) || ',') > 0  AND instr(',' || '{{interpreter_names}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(process_name) || ',') > 0  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)

    What a hit looks like. Outlook or Thunderbird spawning interpreters. Silence indicates no macro-style execution was caught by the process sensor.

  7. Agent triage

    Synthesize full BEC assessment

    Consolidate identity triage results with follow-on persistence and execution evidence.

  8. Decision

    Route based on BEC verdict

    Direct the workflow to containment or manual investigation based on agent confidence.

  9. Response action

    Contain and remediate compromise

    Halt the attack by revoking sessions, isolating endpoints, and removing malicious rules.

  10. Analyst task

    Manual analyst review

    Verify if financial damage occurred and audit for sensitive data exfiltration.

  11. Analyst task

    Hunt close-out

    Finalize findings and hand off logic for standing detection.

# Identity-Centric Business Email Compromise

This hunt follows the full lifecycle of a Business Email Compromise (BEC) attack using a phased approach. It first identifies early-stage risk: successful logins from anonymizing proxies and endpoint traffic to known phishing landing pages. It then pivots to look for follow-on persistence through M365 mailbox rule modifications and potential payload execution where the attacker uses a compromised account to deliver malicious attachments to internal peers. Two agents weigh the evidence sequentially to distinguish between legitimate administrative activity and a coordinated intrusion.
