Post-Compromise Evasion and Ransomware Impact

Medium severity TLP:CLEAR Part 2 of 2 Research by ESET Research Published 3 Nov 2026

Hypothesis

An adversary is using signed system binaries to proxy execution of infostealers or ransomware, transitioning from stealthy credential harvesting to mass file encryption.

Brief

Why this hunt

ESET recently highlighted the confusion SMBs face during security incidents in their article, When confusion reigns, trusted vendors must guide their SMB customers (https://www.welivesecurity.com/en/business-security/when-confusion-reigns-trusted-vendors-guide-smb-customers/). Small and medium-sized businesses often lack specialized resources to distinguish between administrative tool use and sophisticated post-compromise activity. This hunt addresses that gap by focusing on how adversaries transition from stealthy defense evasion to material impact.

How the hunt flows

The first step uses a lead query on the hb_process_activity surface. It identifies instances of signed Microsoft binaries being used to execute code or fetch content. The query specifically monitors binaries like mshta.exe, certutil.exe, and regsvr32.exe for command lines containing network protocols like http or ftp. This step provides a list of suspicious hosts that require deeper investigation. The second phase runs two queries in parallel, scoped strictly to the hosts identified in the lead. The first parallel branch examines the hb_file_activity surface for access to sensitive credential stores. It looks for processes reading files such as browser cookies, login data, or master keys. By limiting this search to the suspicious hosts, we minimize the false positives generated by legitimate profile synchronization or backup software. The second parallel branch monitors the same suspicious hosts for signs of ransomware. It calculates the velocity of file modifications and searches for common ransom note filenames like readme.txt or decrypt.txt. A process touching more than 500 unique files within the lookback window suggests active encryption. This dual-track analysis allows the hunt to catch both infostealer activity and the early stages of a ransomware outbreak. Finally, the hunt uses an automated triage step to correlate the LOLBAS execution with the observed file impacts. This triage produces a per-host verdict that links the evasion technique to a concrete impact. A malicious verdict is only reached if a host shows both the suspicious binary execution and either credential access or mass file activity.

What the hunt cannot see

This hunt has specific blind spots. It cannot identify mass encryption if the adversary targets unmonitored disks or network mounts that the agent does not track. Additionally, sophisticated infostealers that read credentials directly from memory, such as by scraping LSASS, will not be caught by the file-based credential check.

Steps

  1. Query, detection candidate

    Suspicious System Binary Proxy Execution

    Find instances of signed Microsoft binaries being used to execute code or fetch content, which often precedes credential theft or ransomware deployment.

    sql, reads hb_process_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, process_name, process_cmd_line, parent_process_name, user_name, timeFROM hb_process_activityWHERE instr(',' || '{{lolbas_paths}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(process_path) || ',') > 0  AND (LOWER(process_cmd_line) LIKE '%http%' OR LOWER(process_cmd_line) LIKE '%ftp%')  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. Trusted binaries behaving as downloaders. Silence suggests no obvious LOLBAS abuse in this window.

  2. Query, enrichment

    Access to Credential Stores

    Identify processes reading browser password and session stores, limited to suspicious hosts to ensure findings are actionable.

    sql, reads hb_file_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, process_name, file_name, file_path, timeFROM hb_file_activityWHERE activity_id IN (2, 14)  AND instr(',' || '{{credential_filenames}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(file_name) || ',') > 0  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. Anomalous processes accessing sensitive credential files. Silence suggests no secondary file impact on those machines.

  3. Query, baseline

    Mass File Activity and Ransom Notes

    Find high-velocity file modifications or ransom note creation scoped to the suspicious hosts.

    sql, reads hb_file_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, process_name, COUNT(DISTINCT file_path) AS file_count, MIN(time) AS first_seenFROM hb_file_activityWHERE (activity_id IN (1, 3, 5) OR instr(',' || '{{ransom_note_filenames}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(file_name) || ',') > 0)  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY device_hostname, process_nameHAVING file_count > 500  OR MAX(CASE WHEN instr(',' || '{{ransom_note_filenames}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(file_name) || ',') > 0 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) = 1

    What a hit looks like. A process touching many unique files or dropping a ransom note. Silence indicates no active mass encryption.

  4. Agent triage

    Triage Intrusion Evidence

    Correlate the LOLBAS lead with the observed file impacts to settle on a verdict.

  5. Decision

    Route on Verdict

    Take immediate action on malicious findings or route to an analyst for deeper review.

  6. Response action

    Isolate Malicious Endpoint

    Prevent ransomware spread and halt credential theft immediately.

  7. Analyst task

    Analyst Forensic Review

    Validate the findings and investigate the scope of the incident.

  8. Analyst task

    Remediation and Close Out

    Finalize remediation and tune the hunt for future runs.

Blind spots

  • Needs hb_file_activity on all drives. An adversary could encrypt shared network drives from a machine without an agent, or on a drive excluded from monitoring, causing the encryption check to return zero rows. It would answer: Can we see mass encryption if it happens on an unmonitored disk or network mount?
  • Needs hb_process_activity showing handle access to LSASS. Sophisticated infostealers that read from memory (LSASS) rather than files will not be identified by the credential-access-check. It would answer: Are credentials stolen directly from memory without touching the browser files?

# Post-Compromise Evasion and Ransomware Impact

This hunt identifies the progression from defense evasion to destructive impact. It begins by identifying LOLBAS binaries performing suspicious network requests. The hunt then fans out to investigate whether these specific hosts show signs of credential theft or high-velocity file encryption. By scoping the impact queries to the suspicious hosts identified in the lead, the hunt distinguishes malicious activity from fleet-wide background noise and administrative maintenance.

## ransomware-activity-check
<!-- Mass File Activity and Ransom Notes -->
Find high-velocity file modifications or ransom note creation scoped to the suspicious hosts.

```sqlite target=endpoint role=baseline params=(ransom_note_filenames=ransom_note_filenames, scope_hosts=scope_hosts, lookback_days=lookback_days)
~~~yaml

  compare: first_seen
  window: '{{lookback_days}}d'
expected: A process touching many unique files or dropping a ransom note. Silence
  indicates no active mass encryption.
prevalence:
  by: device_hostname
  key:
  - process_name
  rare_below: 5
reads:
- device_hostname
- process_name
- file_name
- file_path
- activity_id
- time
silence: not_evidence_of_absence
source: hb_file_activity

SELECT device_hostname, process_name, COUNT(DISTINCT file_path) AS file_count, MIN(time) AS first_seen FROM hb_file_activity WHERE (activity_id IN (1, 3, 5) OR instr(',' || '{{ransom_note_filenames}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(file_name) || ',') > 0) AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY device_hostname, process_name HAVING file_count > 500 OR MAX(CASE WHEN instr(',' || '{{ransom_note_filenames}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(file_name) || ',') > 0 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) = 1
```
