TGR-STA-0049: QUIC C2 and Custom RAT Operations

An intruder is using HTTP/3 (QUIC) over UDP 443 for command and control and loading custom RAT modules to perform data collection, while masking their activity with synthetic background noise.

Brief

Context

Recent research from Unit 42 titled QUIC and Covert: Novel HTTP/3-Powered RATs Target Southeast Asian Governments details the advanced tradecraft of TGR-STA-0049, a cyberespionage group targeting strategic government entities. The group stands out for its early adoption of HTTP/3 (QUIC) as a stealthy command and control protocol. This choice allows them to bypass traditional network security stacks that are not yet equipped to perform deep packet inspection on UDP-based web traffic.

Hunt Flow

The hunt begins by identifying the potential beachhead. We scope high-value internet-facing infrastructure, specifically Exchange, IIS, nginx, and Apache servers, to establish the primary attack surface. This scoping step focuses the subsequent high-volume telemetry queries on the hosts most likely to be targeted for initial exploitation based on the group's known preferences. The second phase searches for C2 evidence using two parallel tracks. The first track looks for DNS resolutions to known malicious domains identified in the research. The second track performs a behavioral search for any non-browser processes communicating over UDP 443. This is a critical pivot; identifying QUIC traffic from an unexpected process is a strong indicator of protocol tunneling or a custom RAT. An analyst triages these findings to confirm which hosts show active, unauthorized C2 activity. Once the hunt identifies a potential beachhead, we look for indicators of the post-exploitation lifecycle. This involves searching for internal SMB and port scanning activity, which suggests the deployment of the actor's hostscan or SmbBrute modules. Simultaneously, the hunt checks for unauthorized process access to LSASS memory, a signature behavior of the group's credential harvesting tradecraft. By correlating these endpoint events with the initial network findings, we can reach a high-confidence verdict despite the actor's attempts at camouflage.

Blind Spots

The primary network-layer blind spot is the visibility gap within the encrypted QUIC stream. If a network sensor cannot parse HTTP/3 over UDP, the specific commands and hashed endpoints remain invisible at the network layer. On the host, the group's synthetic noise engine is designed to drown out malicious activity within a sea of benign-looking events. Success depends on the analyst's ability to distinguish these RAT-generated artifacts from legitimate system automation using a well-defined heuristic baseline.

# TGR-STA-0049: QUIC C2 and Custom RAT Operations

This hunt targets the cyberespionage tradecraft of TGR-STA-0049, focusing on their adoption of HTTP/3 as a stealthy communication protocol and the deployment of the KUKURAT and PerfectPioneer RAT families. The hunt follows a phased approach: first, it identifies high-value internet-facing servers showing signs of behavioral QUIC C2 traffic or DNS resolutions to the report's indicators. Second, it pivots to investigate post-exploitation behavior including internal SMB scanning and credential harvesting from LSASS memory, reaching a final verdict on the host's compromise state despite the actor's heuristic camouflage.
