Brief

Why This Hunt Matters

The Proofpoint report, "Healthcare Sees No Relief from Cyberattacks Disrupting Patient Care" (https://www.proofpoint.com/us/newsroom/press-releases/cyber-insecurity-in-healthcare-report-2026), highlights how initial access via phishing and supply chain compromises leads to service outages. These disruptions directly impact patient safety. We designed this hunt to identify the specific footprint of these disruptions before they reach the point of total service failure.

Scoping the Clinical Fleet

The hunt begins by identifying the most critical assets. The first query searches software inventory for specific clinical packages like Epic, Cerner, or Meditech. By isolating Electronic Health Record (EHR) servers and diagnostic workstations, the hunt focuses telemetry analysis where the risk to patient care is highest.

Verifying Initial Access

Once scoped, the hunt runs two parallel checks. One query examines authentication logs for successful sign-ins that bypassed multi-factor authentication or originated from unusual geographic locations. Simultaneously, another query looks for rare process executions on these clinical hosts. This identifies potential supply chain tampering where an adversary disguises malware as a legitimate system utility or updater.

Assessing Clinical Impact

The hunt then pivots to find evidence of the intrusion's goal. It monitors outbound network traffic for large data transfers to destinations that are rare across the fleet. At the same time, it looks for file activity bursts, where a single process modifies or deletes thousands of files in a short window. This pattern indicates active data wiping or ransomware encryption.

Correlating the Lifecycle

An analyst or agent assesses these findings together. Instead of alerting on a single rare process, the hunt correlates access, exfiltration, and impact signals. This lifecycle approach helps distinguish legitimate administrative maintenance from a coordinated attack on healthcare operations.

Blind Spots

This hunt has two primary limitations. First, if an adversary uses deep process nesting, the EDR telemetry might truncate the ancestry, hiding the original injection point. Second, while the hunt detects high-volume network transfers, it cannot inspect the contents of encrypted HTTPS traffic. Data leaked via AI prompts or specific PII/PHI transfers remains invisible at the network connection level.

How to Run the Hunt

This hunt is an open-source playbook in the hunt.md format. You can import it into Huntbase or any compatible runtime. It follows a phased flow: it first defines the scope and then executes targeted evidence gathering across authentication, process, network, and file surfaces.

    What a hit looks like. A list of hosts running clinical software. Silence means no matching packages were found in inventory.

  2. Phishing and BEC sign-in check

    Query · baseline

    Identify successful sign-ins to clinical hosts that bypassed MFA or originated from unusual sources.

    reads hb_auth_signinsql
    SELECT device_hostname, actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, src_location_country, mfa, time FROM hb_auth_signin WHERE status_id = 1 AND (mfa = 'false' OR mfa IS NULL) AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') ORDER BY time DESC

    What a hit looks like. A concentration of MFA-less logins on a single host. Rare source countries indicate potential account takeover.

  3. Clinical supply chain process check

    Query · detection candidate

    Find rare processes on the scoped clinical hosts, which may indicate a supply-chain compromise where malware is disguised as normal system operations.

    reads hb_process_activitysql
    SELECT device_hostname, process_path, process_name, parent_process_name, process_cmd_line, COUNT(*) as occurrence_count FROM hb_process_activity WHERE ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY device_hostname, process_path HAVING occurrence_count < 3 ORDER BY occurrence_count ASC

    What a hit looks like. A rare binary path on a host that belongs to the clinical scope. Absence suggests only common fleet-wide binaries are running.

  4. Early stage triage

    Agent triage

    Assess whether any clinical hosts show concurrent signs of access compromise before proceeding to search for impact.

  5. Exfiltration network check

    Query · enrichment

    Identify high-volume outbound network traffic to rare destinations from clinical hosts that may indicate data exfiltration.

    reads hb_network_connectionsql
    SELECT device_hostname, dst_endpoint_ip, traffic_bytes, process_name, time FROM hb_network_connection WHERE direction = 'outbound' AND traffic_bytes > {{large_transfer_threshold}} AND state_kind = 'log' AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') ORDER BY traffic_bytes DESC

    What a hit looks like. Outbound flows exceeding 100MB to rare destination IPs. Absence of rows means no single flow met the threshold or all targets are fleet-wide common.

  6. Ransomware file burst

    Query · triage

    Detect mass file modifications (renames or deletions) on clinical hosts that indicate ransomware encryption activity.

    reads hb_file_activitysql
    SELECT device_hostname, process_name, activity_name, COUNT(*) as file_count, MIN(time) as burst_start FROM hb_file_activity WHERE activity_id IN (3, 4, 5) AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY device_hostname, process_name, activity_name HAVING file_count > 1000 ORDER BY file_count DESC

    What a hit looks like. A single process modifying thousands of files. A hit suggests active encryption or data wiping.

  7. Final intrusion assessment

    Agent triage

    Correlate early access signals with impact evidence to confirm a full intrusion chain threatening patient care.

  8. Route on verdict

    Decision

    Direct the response based on the agent's final assessment of the intrusion status.

  9. Isolate host

    Response action

    Immediately contain the suspected intrusion to prevent further exfiltration or encryption of clinical data.

  10. Analyst validation

    Analyst task

    Perform manual review of the cited evidence to confirm the intrusion chain and update hunt parameters.

  11. Close out

    Analyst task

    Finalize the hunt by documenting results and any identified coverage gaps.

# Healthcare Service Disruption and Data Exfiltration

This hunt follows the attack lifecycle identified in the 2026 Cyber Insecurity in Healthcare report. It identifies critical healthcare assets and then monitors for signs of initial access, such as Business Email Compromise and supply chain anomalies. The hunt then pivots to detect high-volume exfiltration and the mass file encryption characteristic of ransomware, providing a phased assessment of the intrusion's progression on clinical hosts.
