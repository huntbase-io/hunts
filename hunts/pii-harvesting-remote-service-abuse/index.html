PII Harvesting and Remote Service Abuse

Medium severity TLP:CLEAR Research by ESET Research Published 18 Nov 2026

Hypothesis

An intruder has redirected users to a copycat visa or identity portal to harvest passport scans and selfies, then used the stolen identities to authenticate to external remote services from unusual locations.

Brief

Why this hunt

ESET Research, "Is it safe to upload my passport or ID online?", explores how attackers trick users into submitting sensitive identification. This hunt addresses the risk where attackers use copycat portals to harvest passport scans and selfies. We focus on the sequence where a user interacts with a fake portal and then experiences unauthorized access to corporate resources.

How the Hunt Flows

The first phase identifies leads through DNS activity. The lead query finds rare resolutions to domains that mimic legitimate government or travel portals. While we provide a list of identity-themed domains as a baseline, the hunt specifically highlights lookups that are infrequent within the environment. This rarity helps filter out common traffic and isolates potential victims. After an analyst or agent evaluates the legitimacy of these domains, the hunt proceeds to a scouting phase. This phase runs two queries in parallel to gather evidence of both data submission and subsequent abuse. The first query monitors outbound HTTP POST requests. It looks for body sizes exceeding 100KB, which is a proxy for the transmission of image files or scanned documents. Since passport scans are relatively large, this threshold eliminates smaller, routine web form submissions. The second parallel query examines authentication logs for the hosts identified in the DNS phase. It searches for successful sign-ins to remote services that originate from unfamiliar countries or use unexpected protocols. This step identifies the transition from identity theft to active exploitation of the stolen credentials. We prioritize remote interactive and network logons that align with the timing of the initial DNS lead. In the final triage phase, an analyst correlates these signals. A confirmed malicious chain shows a user visiting a suspicious domain, followed by a significant data upload, and ending with anomalous remote access. This progression provides the context necessary to distinguish a targeted attack from routine web browsing. The correlation minimizes the false positives inherent in looking at authentication anomalies or large uploads in isolation.

Blind Spots

This hunt has two primary limitations. If an adversary uses DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH) or a non-enrolled resolver, the initial DNS lead remains invisible. The hunt relies on endpoint or resolver logs to catch the redirection. Furthermore, without TLS inspection on the HTTP traffic, we cannot inspect the contents of the POST requests. We rely on the request body size as a proxy for biometric or document uploads, which may produce false positives from other encrypted data transfers.

Steps

  1. Query, baseline

    DNS lookups to identity copycat domains

    Identify hosts that visited domains mimicking legitimate visa or identity portals.

    sql, reads hb_dns_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, query_hostname, COUNT(*) AS lookup_count, MIN(time) AS first_seenFROM hb_dns_activityWHERE instr(',' || '{{identity_themed_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(query_hostname) || ',') > 0  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY device_hostname, query_hostnameORDER BY lookup_count ASC

    What a hit looks like. A small number of hosts visiting a domain that is visually or semantically similar to a government portal. Silence suggests no interaction with these specific copycats.

  2. Agent triage

    Evaluate domain legitimacy

    Determine if any domains visited in the lead query appear to be illegitimate harvesting portals.

  3. Decision

    Gate: Proceed with expensive scouting?

    Stop the hunt early if no suspicious portal interaction is confirmed.

  4. Query, enrichment

    Potential PII uploads via HTTP POST

    Find large outbound POST requests that may represent passport scans or biometric uploads.

    sql, reads hb_http_activity
    SELECT device_hostname, url_hostname, url_path, request_body_len, timeFROM hb_http_activityWHERE http_method = 'POST'  AND request_body_len > {{upload_min_bytes}}  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. POST requests over 100KB to suspicious or rare domains following the DNS lead hit. Silence suggests no large data was transmitted to the suspected portals.

  5. Query, detection candidate

    Anomalous remote service sign-ins

    Identify unauthorized access to gateways using credentials likely stolen via the harvesting site.

    sql, reads hb_auth_signin
    SELECT device_hostname, actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, src_location_country, logon_type, status, timeFROM hb_auth_signinWHERE (logon_type IN ('Remote Interactive', 'Network') OR activity_id = 5)  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')ORDER BY time DESC

    What a hit looks like. Sign-ins from unfamiliar locations or unusual protocols for the users identified in the scoping phase.

  6. Agent triage

    Correlate harvesting and access anomalies

    Verify if the DNS visit, HTTP upload, and auth anomalies form a single attack chain per host.

  7. Decision

    Route based on compromise

    Initiate containment for confirmed malicious chains.

  8. Response action

    Isolate host and revoke session

    Prevent further unauthorized access and stop potential data exfiltration.

  9. Analyst task

    Analyst manual review

    Verify the correlated chain and assess if additional PII was leaked.

  10. Analyst task

    Close out

    Document findings and update blocklists.

# PII Harvesting and Remote Service Abuse

This hunt identifies the progression from initial phishing redirection to credential abuse. It starts by finding rare DNS lookups to domains that mimic legitimate travel or identity portals. If an agent confirms these domains are suspicious, the hunt scopes to those hosts to find large HTTP POST requests (indicative of image uploads) and subsequent authentication anomalies on VPN or Citrix gateways. An analyst then weighs the complete chain to confirm a compromise.

## dns-leads
<!-- DNS lookups to identity copycat domains -->
Identify hosts that visited domains mimicking legitimate visa or identity portals.

```sqlite target=endpoint role=baseline params=(identity_themed_domains=identity_themed_domains, lookback_days=lookback_days)
~~~yaml
baseline:
  compare: first_seen
  window: '{{lookback_days}}d'
expected: A small number of hosts visiting a domain that is visually or semantically
  similar to a government portal. Silence suggests no interaction with these specific
  copycats.
prevalence:
  by: device_hostname
  key:
  - query_hostname
  rare_below: 5
reads:
- device_hostname
- query_hostname
- time
silence: not_evidence_of_absence
source: hb_dns_activity
verified: dry-run
verified_at: '2026-11-18'
~~~
SELECT device_hostname, query_hostname, COUNT(*) AS lookup_count, MIN(time) AS first_seen FROM hb_dns_activity WHERE instr(',' || '{{identity_themed_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(query_hostname) || ',') > 0 AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY device_hostname, query_hostname ORDER BY lookup_count ASC
```

## final-triage
<!-- Correlate harvesting and access anomalies -->
```agent target=hunter
cite: required
context:
- evaluate-leads
- pii-upload-check
- remote-access-check
max_iterations: 6
objective: Determine if users who visited the suspicious portals also performed large
  uploads and subsequently experienced anomalous remote authentication events.
success_criteria: A per-host verdict of malicious | suspicious | benign citing specific
  DNS, HTTP, and Auth rows.
tools:
- endpoint
- identity
- web
```

<!-- Route based on compromise -->
if~: "the final-triage verdict is malicious for at least one host and user" (confidence: high, judge=hunter)

## contain-and-reset
<!-- Isolate host and revoke session -->
```action target=endpoint
~~~yaml
approval: required
~~~
Isolate the identified host and revoke all active cloud/VPN sessions for the affected user. Force a password reset.
```
→ analyst-review

## analyst-review
<!-- Analyst manual review -->
```manual target=analyst
Review the DNS query times against the HTTP POST request times. Confirm if the remote access originated from a known malicious IP or unusual geographic region.
```
→ end

## close-out
<!-- Close out -->
```manual target=analyst
Record the identified malicious domains for perimeter blocking and document the host/user combinations examined.
```
→ end
