SSO Takeover and Data Impact

An adversary has bypassed SSO protections using stolen credentials and is now performing bulk data exfiltration or deploying ransomware across the environment.

Brief

Why this hunt?

Cisco Talos recently detailed the mechanics of identity-centric intrusions in their report, "The story behind the intelligence" (https://blog.talosintelligence.com/the-story-behind-the-intelligence/). Adversaries increasingly use vishing to bypass MFA or trick users into approving push notifications. Once inside the SSO provider, they have the keys to the entire cloud and on-premises kingdom. This hunt provides a structured way to find those who have already breached the perimeter.

Scoping the Identity Lead

The hunt begins at the identity provider. The first phase queries the hb_auth_signin surface to identify successful Okta logins where the provider recorded no multi-factor authentication. This scoping query filters out the majority of legitimate traffic, focusing the investigation on accounts that might have been compromised through session hijacking or legacy protocol exploitation. This list of users and their associated source hosts provides the starting point for the rest of the hunt.

Identifying Concurrent Impact

Once the hunt identifies potential account takeovers, it branches into a parallel search for malicious intent. One branch monitors hb_cloud_api_activity for mass data access. It looks for users who execute over 1,000 read operations within the lookback window. This behavior often indicates automated scraping of S3 buckets or mailboxes. The query looks for high-volume API calls that deviate from standard administrative patterns. The second branch focuses on the endpoint via hb_process_activity. It searches the hosts identified in the login phase for two specific indicators: the use of known ransomware utilities like vssadmin.exe to delete shadow copies, and the presence of the specific "azim sucks" string in command lines. This string is a known marker from specific threat actors described in recent intelligence. By searching for these markers on hosts tied to weak logins, the hunt narrows the focus to active threats.

Correlating Evidence

A triage agent ingests the results from both branches. It evaluates whether the users identified in the MFA-less login phase are the same actors performing bulk cloud reads or executing suspicious processes. This correlation is critical. Detecting a new login location is often a false positive, but a new login followed by 5,000 API calls for data access constitutes a high-confidence incident. The agent issues a per-host verdict to guide the final analyst review or automated isolation.

Blind Spots and Limitations

This hunt focuses on the "how" of the impact, not the "how" of the initial lure. It cannot see the vishing call or the social engineering interaction because it lacks access to VOIP or corporate phone logs. Additionally, if an adversary operates on a host without EDR coverage or process logging, the ransomware markers remain invisible. The hunt relies on hb_process_activity having command-line support to see the actor-specific strings.

Running the Playbook

This hunt exists as an open hunt.md playbook. You can import it into Huntbase or any runtime that supports the hunt.md standard. The playbook includes the necessary parameters to adjust the lookback window and refine the list of impact markers based on your environment's baseline. It provides a repeatable workflow to verify the integrity of your SSO sessions and detect high-impact data theft or system destruction.

Steps

  1. Identify MFA-less Okta sign-ins

    Query · scoping

    Identify successful Okta logins where MFA was not utilized, establishing a lead for potential account takeover using stolen credentials.

    reads hb_auth_signinsql
    SELECT device_hostname, actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, event_type, mfa, time FROM hb_auth_signin WHERE provider = 'okta' AND status_id = 1 AND (mfa IS NULL OR mfa = 'false' OR mfa = 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. A list of hostnames and usernames that successfully authenticated without MFA. Silence means every successful Okta login during the window recorded an MFA check.

  2. Detect bulk cloud read operations

    Query · baseline

    Find users performing an unusually high volume of read operations, which is indicative of automated exfiltration following an SSO takeover.

    reads hb_cloud_api_activitysql
    SELECT actor_user_name, api_operation, COUNT(*) AS call_count, MIN(time) AS first_seen FROM hb_cloud_api_activity WHERE activity_id = 2 AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY actor_user_name, api_operation HAVING call_count > 1000 ORDER BY call_count DESC

    What a hit looks like. Specific accounts making thousands of read requests in a short window. Silence indicates no mass-read behavior was recorded.

  3. Ransomware behavioral markers

    Query · detection candidate

    Identify process execution that matches common ransomware patterns or contains actor-specific strings on the scoped hosts.

    reads hb_process_activitysql
    SELECT device_hostname, user_name, process_name, process_cmd_line, time FROM hb_process_activity WHERE ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND (instr(',' || '{{impact_markers}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(process_name) || ',') > 0 OR LOWER(process_cmd_line) LIKE '%azim%sucks%') AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. Rows containing ransomware utility execution or the specific azim sucks string. Silence suggests no such markers were observed on the specified hosts.

  4. Weigh sign-in and impact evidence

    Agent triage

    Correlate the MFA-less identity leads with subsequent cloud exfiltration or ransomware execution markers.

  5. Route on triage verdict

    Decision

    Direct the response based on the agent's confidence in the identity-to-impact intrusion chain.

  6. Isolate compromised host

    Response action

    Prevent further encryption or data exfiltration by removing the confirmed beachhead from the network.

  7. Analyst forensic review

    Analyst task

    Verify the agent's correlation and perform manual investigation into the specific objects accessed in the cloud.

  8. Close out hunt

    Analyst task

    Record final dispositions and document telemetry gaps identified during the hunt.

# SSO Takeover and Data Impact

This hunt investigates the critical sequence from identity compromise to high-impact data operations. It starts by identifying successful Okta sign-ins that occurred without MFA, establishing a baseline of potentially compromised accounts and the hosts used for access. The hunt then branches into two parallel investigations of adversary intent: mass data access in cloud environments and the presence of ransomware-specific process markers on the identified hosts. An agent evaluates the combined evidence to determine if the sign-ins correlate with malicious impact, specifically looking for actor-specific strings like azim sucks and bulk record theft.
