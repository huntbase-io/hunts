VPN Entry and Internal Brute Force

Brief

Why this hunt

Unit 42 recently detailed an intrusion in "The Noise Inside the Network: How an Unsophisticated Attack Exposed a Third-Party Risk" (https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/noise-inside-network-third-party-risk/). The report highlights how attackers exploit weak perimeter security on third-party access points. This hunt replicates that detection path. We look for the moment an adversary enters via a VPN and immediately begins testing credentials to move laterally.

How the hunt flows

The scoping phase targets the perimeter. The first query identifies successful sign-ins to VPN services where multi-factor authentication was either disabled or not reported. This narrows the focus to high-risk sessions that bypass standard security controls.

The second phase fans out to find evidence of lateral risk. One query monitors for internal brute-force patterns. It identifies internal source IPs attempting to guess passwords for multiple administrative accounts, such as root or service accounts. Simultaneously, the hunt baselines account lifecycle changes. It looks for rare password resets or account creations that deviate from normal administrative behavior.

The triage phase correlates these signals. An agent evaluates the coincidence of the MFA-less VPN entry, the volume of internal authentication failures, and any anomalous account modifications. This determines if the activity indicates a successful intrusion or routine network noise.

What this hunt cannot see

This hunt has two primary blind spots. First, it depends on VPN gateway logs residing in the central data lake. If an attacker enters through an unmonitored or shadow gateway, the scoping step will fail. Second, the hunt relies on the accuracy of MFA reporting. If a provider always reports MFA status as NULL or if the ingestion parser does not correctly map the field, the hunt will generate excessive false positives.

Why this is a hunt

We designed this as a hunt because a single detection rule typically focuses on one signal, such as a brute-force attempt. That approach lacks context. This hunt correlates the entry point with the subsequent volume of failures and the baseline of account changes. By looking at the identity plane holistically, we distinguish between a noisy scanner and a specific adversary following a perimeter breach.

Steps

  1. VPN Sign-ins Without MFA

    Query · scoping

    Identify successful logins to VPN services that did not use or report multi-factor authentication.

    reads hb_auth_signinsql
    SELECT DISTINCT device_hostname, actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, service_name, time FROM hb_auth_signin WHERE status_id = 1 AND (mfa = 'false' OR mfa IS NULL) AND (LOWER(service_name) LIKE '%vpn%' OR LOWER(dst_endpoint_name) LIKE '%vpn%' OR LOWER(metadata_product) LIKE '%vpn%') AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. Rows naming the VPN gateway and the users who successfully logged in without MFA. Silence proves no MFA-less logins were recorded for gateways reporting to the lake.

  2. Internal Brute Force Against Admins

    Query · detection candidate

    Find source IPs attempting to guess passwords for multiple administrative accounts.

    reads hb_auth_signinsql
    SELECT src_endpoint_ip, device_hostname, COUNT(DISTINCT actor_user_name) AS unique_accounts, COUNT(*) AS attempt_count FROM hb_auth_signin WHERE status_id = 2 AND (LOWER(actor_user_name) LIKE '%admin%' OR LOWER(actor_user_name) LIKE '%root%' OR instr(',' || '{{admin_account_patterns}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(actor_user_name) || ',') > 0) AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY src_endpoint_ip, device_hostname HAVING attempt_count > 20

    What a hit looks like. A single internal IP address generating dozens of authentication failures across different admin names. Silence suggests no automated guessing was visible on these hosts.

  3. Rare Account Lifecycle Changes

    Query · baseline

    Baseline account changes to identify rare password resets or creations that could indicate persistence.

    reads hb_account_changesql
    SELECT user_name, activity_name, device_hostname, COUNT(DISTINCT actor_user_name) AS distinct_actors, MIN(time) AS first_change FROM hb_account_change WHERE activity_id IN (1, 4) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY user_name, activity_name, device_hostname

    What a hit looks like. A password reset or account creation performed by an unusual actor. Baseline noise will show routine resets; anomalous entries will have low actor counts.

# VPN Entry and Internal Brute Force

This hunt examines the identity plane for signs of third-party risk and credential abuse. It starts by scoping for successful VPN sign-ins where multi-factor authentication (MFA) was not enforced. It then fans out to identify internal brute-force patterns targeting administrative accounts and correlates these with rare account lifecycle changes, such as password resets or account creations. An agent weighs the combined evidence to distinguish between automated noisy scanners and a successful intrusion originating from a compromised VPN session.

## vpn-entry-no-mfa
<!-- VPN Sign-ins Without MFA -->
Identify successful logins to VPN services that did not use or report multi-factor authentication.

```sqlite target=identity role=scoping params=(lookback_days=lookback_days)
~~~yaml
expected: Rows naming the VPN gateway and the users who successfully logged in without
  MFA. Silence proves no MFA-less logins were recorded for gateways reporting to the
  lake.
reads:
- device_hostname
- actor_user_name
- src_endpoint_ip
- service_name
- dst_endpoint_name
- mfa
- status_id
- time
- metadata_product
silence: not_evidence_of_absence
source: hb_auth_signin
verified: dry-run
verified_at: '2026-10-14'
~~~
SELECT DISTINCT device_hostname, actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, service_name, time FROM hb_auth_signin WHERE status_id = 1 AND (mfa = 'false' OR mfa IS NULL) AND (LOWER(service_name) LIKE '%vpn%' OR LOWER(dst_endpoint_name) LIKE '%vpn%' OR LOWER(metadata_product) LIKE '%vpn%') AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')
```

## unusual-account-changes
<!-- Rare Account Lifecycle Changes -->
Baseline account changes to identify rare password resets or creations that could indicate persistence.

```sqlite target=endpoint role=baseline params=(lookback_days=lookback_days)
~~~yaml
baseline:
  compare: first_seen
  window: '{{lookback_days}}d'
expected: A password reset or account creation performed by an unusual actor. Baseline
  noise will show routine resets; anomalous entries will have low actor counts.
prevalence:
  by: device_hostname
  key:
  - user_name
  - activity_name
  rare_below: 3
reads:
- user_name
- activity_name
- device_hostname
- activity_id
- actor_user_name
- time
silence: not_evidence_of_absence
source: hb_account_change

```
