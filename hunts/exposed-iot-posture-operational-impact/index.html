Exposed IoT Posture and Operational Impact

Medium severity TLP:CLEAR Part 1 of 2 Research by ESET Research Published 21 Nov 2026

Hypothesis

An adversary has exploited unpatched vulnerabilities in end-of-life IoT devices, evidenced by rare process execution on identified smart hardware, unauthorized external exposure of management ports, and failing states in the IoT control plane.

Brief

Why now

Unmanaged smart hardware often lacks the security updates necessary to withstand modern exploits. In a recent analysis, ESET Research — My IoT devices are no longer supported – is my smart home safe? details how end-of-life IoT devices represent a permanent and unpatchable attack surface. These devices frequently sit in sensitive network segments yet receive little to no monitoring once the manufacturer stops providing support. This hunt provides a structured way to identify these devices and verify their integrity.

How the hunt flows

The hunt begins by scoping the enrolled estate for devices that identify as IoT hardware. It uses a keyword-based query against the local device inventory to find hosts with names or hardware vendors matching profiles like cameras, gateways, and controllers. This ensures the subsequent, more intensive behavioral checks focus only on relevant hardware.

Once scoped, the hunt fans out to correlate evidence across three independent surfaces. The first query identifies rare processes on the IoT hosts, looking for binaries seen on three or fewer devices. Simultaneously, the hunt checks external asset data for management interfaces like RTSP or HTTP exposed on the public internet. A third check examines the Azure IoT Hub control plane to find hubs in an inactive or failed state, which can signal device-level failures or takeover.

An analyst then triages these combined results. By weighing an internal process anomaly against an external exposure finding, the analyst determines if a device acts as a beachhead. If the hunt confirms a compromise, the workflow provides instructions to isolate the device at the network level and revoke its cloud credentials.

What the hunt cannot see

This hunt relies on existing endpoint and cloud telemetry. If an IoT device does not run an agent or report to the internal inventory, it remains invisible to the process-level checks. Furthermore, while the hunt identifies failed states in the IoT Hub, it cannot see specific malicious commands sent to devices without CloudToDevice diagnostic logging enabled. We recommend passive network monitoring to identify these silent, unmanaged devices by their MAC addresses.

Steps

  1. Query, scoping

    Identify potential IoT inventory

    Find hosts whose hardware vendor or hostname matches known IoT profiles to scope the behavioral hunt.

    sql, reads hb_devices
    SELECT hostname, hardware_vendor, hardware_model, os_name, first_seenFROM hb_devicesWHERE time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || hostname || ',') > 0)  AND (instr(',' || '{{iot_keywords}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(hostname) || ',') > 0 OR instr(',' || '{{iot_keywords}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(hardware_vendor) || ',') > 0)

    What a hit looks like. A list of hosts with IoT-related hardware or naming conventions. Silence indicates no managed IoT devices matching these keywords were found.

  2. Query, baseline

    Rare processes on IoT hosts

    Identify anomalous binaries or unique process paths on the scoped IoT devices that may indicate malware or exploitation.

    sql, reads hb_process_activity
    SELECT LOWER(process_path) AS path, COUNT(DISTINCT device_hostname) AS hosts, COUNT(*) AS runs, MIN(time) AS first_seenFROM hb_process_activityWHERE ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY LOWER(process_path)HAVING hosts <= 3ORDER BY hosts, runs

    What a hit looks like. Binaries seen on only a few IoT hosts. Silence suggests no unusual process execution occurred on the enrolled estate during the window.

  3. Query, enrichment

    Exposed IoT management interfaces

    Identify internet-exposed ports for IoT products that match the identified internal hardware profiles.

    sql, reads hb_exposed_assets
    SELECT domain_or_ip, port, product, version, discovered_atFROM hb_exposed_assetsWHERE (LOWER(product) LIKE '%camera%' OR LOWER(product) LIKE '%dvr%' OR LOWER(product) LIKE '%router%' OR LOWER(product) LIKE '%gateway%')  AND discovered_at >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. External scan results showing management ports (80, 443, 554, 8000) open on your IP space for identified IoT products.

  4. Query, triage

    IoT Hub operational health

    Check the Azure IoT Hub control plane for failed provisioning or inactive states that indicate operational impact.

    sql, reads azure_iothub
    SELECT name, state, provisioning_state, resource_groupFROM azure_iothubWHERE state != 'Active'  OR provisioning_state = 'Failed'

    What a hit looks like. Hubs in a failed or inactive state. This may indicate a control-plane takeover or device-level failure following an exploit.

  5. Agent triage

    Triage IoT risk and compromise

    Weigh the internal process anomalies against external exposure and control plane failures to determine if an IoT device has been compromised.

  6. Decision

    Route on IoT verdict

    Direct the workflow based on the presence of malicious activity.

  7. Response action

    Isolate IoT device

    Prevent lateral movement or further operational impact by isolating the compromised device.

  8. Analyst task

    Firmware and EoL status review

    Verify if the identified devices are officially end-of-life and plan for replacement.

  9. Analyst task

    Operational impact analysis

    Assess whether device failures have impacted physical operations.

# Exposed IoT Posture and Operational Impact

This hunt identifies end-of-life or unmanaged IoT devices and correlates their presence with behavioural and operational anomalies. We scope the estate using device inventory to identify hardware typical of IoT systems using keyword matching on vendors and hostnames. We then fan out to check independent signals: rare binaries executing on these hosts, internet-exposed management interfaces for matching IoT products, and non-active operational states in Azure IoT Hub. An agent weighs these results to detect compromised devices that may be impacting physical operations or acting as a network beachhead.

<!-- Identify potential IoT inventory -->


## rare-iot-processes
<!-- Rare processes on IoT hosts -->
Identify anomalous binaries or unique process paths on the scoped IoT devices that may indicate malware or exploitation.

```sqlite target=endpoint role=baseline params=(lookback_days=lookback_days, scope_hosts=scope_hosts)

  window: '{{lookback_days}}d'
expected: Binaries seen on only a few IoT hosts. Silence suggests no unusual process

  by: device_hostname
  key:
  - process_path
  rare_below: 3

- device_hostname
- process_path
- time

```
