Medicare Open Enrollment Social Engineering

An adversary is using lookalike domains during the Medicare Open Enrollment season to harvest personal and medical data via social engineering, subsequently using those credentials to access sensitive health and banking portals.

Brief

Why this hunt matters

Open Enrollment for Medicare occurs annually between October 15 and December 7. This period creates a surge in legitimate traffic to healthcare portals, which provides an ideal window for adversaries to deploy seasonal lures. A recent report by ESET, Medicare scam calls, texts and ads: What to watch for this Open Enrollment, explains how scammers use deceptive ads and lookalike websites to harvest sensitive personal and medical data. Detecting this activity via simple keyword alerts usually results in overwhelming false positives because legitimate usage shares the same terminology. This hunt addresses the problem by correlating the initial lure visit with downstream data submission and credential monetization.

How the hunt flows

The first phase uses DNS activity to identify potential leads. The hunt queries for any domains containing the words medicare or healthcare that do not end in official .gov TLDs. It also filters out a list of known-good government reporting sites. This scoping step is low-cost and allows an analyst to quickly see which hosts in the environment are interacting with non-official healthcare infrastructure.

After an analyst reviews the DNS leads and identifies suspicious domains, the hunt proceeds to a parallel deep-dive. One branch of this analysis searches HTTP traffic for rare POST requests. It specifically looks for data being sent to domains that contain Medicare keywords but are not part of the legitimate government estate. This captures the moment a user submits sensitive information into a malicious web form.

The second branch of the parallel fan-out monitors authentication logs for those same scoped hosts. It tracks sign-ins to sensitive services including banking, insurance, and medical portals. By correlating a visit to a suspicious domain with a subsequent login to a financial service, the hunt identifies patterns where an adversary may be testing stolen credentials or moving from identity harvesting to monetization.

The final stage synthesizes these findings. An agent correlates the timing of the DNS lure interaction, the HTTP POST submission, and the authentication attempts. This provides a clear picture of the attack chain, allowing the team to distinguish a user who is simply researching health plans from one who has successfully been phished.

What the hunt cannot see

This hunt has two primary blind spots. First, it does not inspect the payload of HTTP POST requests. Without TLS inspection or proxy body logs, an analyst can see that a user sent data to a suspicious site, but cannot confirm the presence of a Medicare number or Social Security Number. Second, the hunt is limited by the retention period of DNS logs. Because social engineering campaigns can persist for weeks, a user might interact with a lure before the current logging window, leaving only the downstream authentication behavior visible to the analyst.

# Medicare Open Enrollment Social Engineering

This hunt identifies seasonal social engineering campaigns targeting Medicare beneficiaries. It uses a gated flow: first, a low-cost DNS scoping step identifies hosts interacting with non-government domains containing Medicare keywords. If an agent confirms these as suspicious lures, the hunt performs an expensive parallel fan-out to analyze HTTP POST data-harvesting patterns and concurrent unauthorized sign-ins to sensitive portals. This approach isolates the transition from the initial lure to the monetization of stolen identity and health data.

## lead-dns-lookups
<!-- Lead DNS lookups for Medicare lures -->
Identify potential social engineering lures by finding DNS queries for domains containing Medicare keywords that do not end in official .gov TLDs.

```sqlite target=endpoint role=scoping params=(lookback_days=lookback_days, legit_domains=legit_domains)
~~~yaml

  Silence suggests no users have visited obvious lures in the window.

SELECT device_hostname, actor_user_name, query_hostname, process_name, time FROM hb_dns_activity WHERE (LOWER(query_hostname) LIKE '%medicare%' OR LOWER(query_hostname) LIKE '%healthcare%') AND LOWER(query_hostname) NOT LIKE '%.gov' AND LOWER(query_hostname) NOT LIKE '%.gov.%' AND instr(',' || '{{legit_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(query_hostname) || ',') = 0 AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')
```

## rare-http-post-targets

Detect potential harvesting behavior by finding rare domains receiving POST requests containing Medicare keywords, filtered to the scoped hosts.

