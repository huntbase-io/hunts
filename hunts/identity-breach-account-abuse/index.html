Identity Breach and Account Abuse

Adversaries are leveraging corporate credentials exposed in third-party data breaches to bypass authentication, propagate phishing lures, and exfiltrate data from internal endpoints.

Brief

Why this hunt

ESET Research recently highlighted the ambiguity of data breach notices in What to do when a data breach notice tells you almost nothing. When a third-party service loses your data, the notice rarely explains if your corporate credentials are already being sold or used against your infrastructure. We developed this hunt to close that gap by turning external intelligence into a targeted internal investigation.

How the hunt flows

The hunt begins by identifying active managed devices through the hb_devices surface. This ensures the follow-on behavioral queries focus on systems currently reporting telemetry, reducing the noise from decommissioned or dormant assets.

Next, the hunt performs a parallel external intelligence check. It queries HaveIBeenPwned (HIBP) and Hudson Rock for any corporate email domains. This phase separates generic historical leaks from high-risk infostealer infections. An analyst or agent then weighs these results to create a list of high-risk identities for the next phase.

The investigation then pivots to internal behavioral surfaces. It examines hb_auth_signin to find successful sign-ins from rare source IPs or unusual geographic locations for the flagged accounts. This identifies successful authentication that may bypass traditional geo-fencing.

Simultaneously, the hunt scans hb_http_activity for web requests containing common phishing or credential-harvesting keywords like "verify" or "office". It also monitors hb_network_connection for outbound transfers exceeding 50MB originating from those same accounts. This three-surface approach helps verify if a compromised account is being used for internal lateral movement or data theft.

Finally, the hunt correlates the timing of the external breach with these internal anomalies. If an account appears in a fresh infostealer log and subsequently shows rare login patterns or high-volume data transfers, the hunt directs the analyst to revoke sessions and reset the identity.

Blind spots

This hunt has two primary limitations. First, without SSL decryption for HTTP traffic, the hunt can identify that a user visited a suspicious domain but cannot confirm if they submitted credentials on a specific URI path. Second, the hunt depends on high-fidelity authentication logs. If the environment lacks full MFA event details, distinguishing between a simple password replay and a sophisticated session token theft is difficult.

Running this hunt

This is a hunt.md playbook. It provides a structured flow that is more complex than a standard detection rule because it requires correlating external exposure with multi-surface behavior. You can import this file into Huntbase or any runtime that supports the hunt.md specification to automate the intelligence gathering and behavioral analysis phases.

    What a hit looks like. HTTP interaction with potential phishing URLs by compromised users. Silence confirms no such lures were visited by these specific accounts.

# Identity Breach and Account Abuse

This phased hunt identifies corporate identities compromised in external breaches and infostealer activity, then monitors those specific accounts for anomalous internal behavior. By correlating external intelligence with authentication, web, and network telemetry, the hunt distinguishes between simple credential exposure and active account weaponization.

## detect-anomalous-logins
<!-- Anomalous logins for high-risk identities -->
Find successful sign-ins from rare source IP addresses specifically for the corporate identities flagged in the intelligence phase.

```sqlite target=identity role=baseline params=(lookback_days=lookback_days, scope_hosts=scope_hosts, high_risk_identities=high_risk_identities)
~~~yaml
baseline:
  compare: first_seen
  window: '{{lookback_days}}d'
expected: Successful logins from unusual IPs or locations for high-risk accounts.
  Silence proves no rare successful authentications occurred for these users.
prevalence:
  by: device_hostname
  key:
  - actor_user_name
  - src_endpoint_ip
  rare_below: 5
reads:
- actor_user_name
- device_hostname
- src_endpoint_ip
- src_location_country
- status_id
- time
silence: not_evidence_of_absence
source: hb_auth_signin
verified: dry-run
verified_at: '2026-10-14'
~~~
SELECT actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, src_location_country, device_hostname, COUNT(*) as login_count, MIN(time) as first_seen FROM hb_auth_signin WHERE status_id = 1 AND ('{{high_risk_identities}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{high_risk_identities}}' || ',', ',' || actor_user_name || ',') > 0) AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0) AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days') GROUP BY actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, src_location_country, device_hostname HAVING login_count < 5 ORDER BY login_count ASC
```
