AI Agent Manipulation and Data Exfiltration

Medium severity TLP:CLEAR Research by Microsoft Published 26 Nov 2026

Hypothesis

An adversary has delivered a prompt-injection email that manipulated a cloud-hosted AI agent into collecting sensitive SharePoint documents and exfiltrating them via outbound email.

Brief

Why now

Microsoft recently highlighted the evolution of email defenses in their post, Microsoft named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner® Magic Quadrant™ for Email Security (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/25/microsoft-named-a-leader-in-the-2026-gartner-magic-quadrant-for-email-security/). As organizations adopt agents like Copilot, the attack surface expands from malicious links to natural language instructions. Attackers use these directives to bypass traditional filters that look for known bad infrastructure.

How the hunt flows

The first phase focuses on scoping and identifying potential triggers. The analyst uses software inventory data from hb_software_inventory to find hosts running modern, AI-integrated versions of Teams and Office. A parallel query on microsoft365_mail_message searches for external emails containing prompt-injection keywords like "ignore previous" or "system prompt." Simultaneously, the hunt checks hb_auth_signin for rare logons to AI services to provide session context.

Once potential triggers are identified, the hunt pivots to cloud audit logs in hb_cloud_api_activity. It searches for rapid file access or previewing in SharePoint and OneDrive specifically performed by the AI service principal or user-agents associated with Copilot. This phase identifies the collection stage where the agent gathers data on behalf of the adversary.

The final phase examines outbound exfiltration. The hunt queries hb_cloud_api_activity for Exchange send operations initiated by the AI agent to external domains. A final correlation step links the timestamp of the suspicious email receipt with the subsequent automated document access and outbound messaging.

What the hunt cannot see

Encrypted emails (S/MIME) hide the injection payload from the initial scan. If an email is deleted before the security tool ingests it, the trigger remains invisible, leaving only the cloud audit aftermath. Additionally, if the audit logs fail to distinguish between a manual user action and an automated agent action, the hunt may generate false positives from legitimate user activity.

Steps

  1. Query, scoping

    Scope hosts with AI-integrated software

    Identify hosts running modern, AI-integrated versions of Teams and Office to narrow the hunt to likely AI users.

    sql, reads hb_software_inventory
    SELECT device_hostname, package_name, package_versionFROM hb_software_inventoryWHERE (LOWER(package_name) = 'microsoft teams' OR LOWER(package_name) LIKE 'microsoft 365%')  AND (package_version LIKE '24%' OR package_version LIKE '25%' OR package_version LIKE '26%')

    What a hit looks like. A list of hosts running AI-capable application versions. Silence implies no modern office suites are installed via standard packaging.

  2. Query, detection candidate

    Email with prompt injection keywords

    Find external emails containing keywords used to override AI agent instructions.

    sql, reads microsoft365_mail_message
    SELECT id, sender, subject, body_preview, received_date_time, tenant_idFROM microsoft365_mail_messageWHERE received_date_time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')  AND (LOWER(body) LIKE '%ignore previous%' OR LOWER(body) LIKE '%system prompt%' OR LOWER(body) LIKE '%financial documents%' OR LOWER(body) LIKE '%forward to %')  AND NOT (instr(',' || '{{internal_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(substr(sender, instr(sender, '@') + 1)) || ',') > 0)

    What a hit looks like. Messages containing directives for AI agents. Silence suggests no plain-text prompt injection attempts matched these keywords.

  3. Query, baseline

    Rare logons to AI-integrated services

    Identify anomalous source IPs for sign-ins to AI interfaces, providing potential session context for an attack.

    sql, reads hb_auth_signin
    SELECT actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, COUNT(DISTINCT device_hostname) AS hosts, MIN(time) AS first_seenFROM hb_auth_signinWHERE (LOWER(dst_endpoint_name) LIKE '%teams%' OR LOWER(dst_endpoint_name) LIKE '%office%' OR LOWER(dst_endpoint_name) LIKE '%copilot%')  AND ('{{scope_hosts}}' = '' OR instr(',' || '{{scope_hosts}}' || ',', ',' || device_hostname || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')GROUP BY actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ipHAVING hosts <= 2

    What a hit looks like. Source IPs seen on only one or two hosts, potentially indicating unauthorized access or proxying. Silence is evidence of absence for rare IP logons to these specific services.

  4. Agent triage

    Evaluate trigger indicators

    Decide if an external email and associated logon pattern indicate a prompt-injection attempt.

  5. Query, enrichment

    Automated AI document collection

    Detect AI agents accessing or downloading multiple sensitive files from SharePoint or OneDrive.

    sql, reads hb_cloud_api_activity
    SELECT actor_user_name, src_endpoint_ip, api_operation, resource_name, time, user_agentFROM hb_cloud_api_activityWHERE api_service_name IN ('SharePoint', 'OneDrive')  AND (LOWER(user_agent) LIKE '%copilot%' OR LOWER(actor_user_name) LIKE '%copilot%')  AND api_operation IN ('FileDownloaded', 'FileAccessed', 'FilePreviewed')  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. A pattern of rapid document access by an AI service principal. Silence suggests no automated file interaction by known AI strings.

  6. Query, enrichment

    Agent-driven outbound email exfiltration

    Detect AI agents sending messages to external domains, potentially carrying gathered documents.

    sql, reads hb_cloud_api_activity
    SELECT actor_user_name, api_operation, target_user_name, time, user_agentFROM hb_cloud_api_activityWHERE api_service_name = 'Exchange'  AND api_operation = 'Send'  AND (LOWER(user_agent) LIKE '%copilot%' OR LOWER(actor_user_name) LIKE '%copilot%')  AND NOT (instr(',' || '{{internal_domains}}' || ',', ',' || LOWER(substr(target_user_name, instr(target_user_name, '@') + 1)) || ',') > 0)  AND time >= datetime('now', '-{{lookback_days}} days')

    What a hit looks like. Outbound email initiated by an AI agent to an external recipient. Silence is evidence that no AI-driven outbound mail occurred to external domains.

  7. Agent triage

    Final full-chain correlation

    Link the trigger email with the automated cloud activity to confirm agent-driven compromise.

  8. Decision

    Route on full-chain verdict

    Direct the hunt based on the severity of the findings.

  9. Response action

    Isolate identity and revoke sessions

    Immediately stop ongoing exfiltration by revoking credentials and agent access.

  10. Analyst task

    Manual analyst review

    Verify the prompt injection content and evaluate the sensitivity of accessed data.

  11. Analyst task

    Close out hunt

    Finalize documentation and ensure tuning of security controls.

# AI Agent Manipulation and Data Exfiltration

This hunt targets the manipulation of AI agents, such as Microsoft Copilot, through hidden prompt injection in emails. Attackers bypass traditional phishing filters by using natural language that contains directives for the AI assistant rather than malicious links. The hunt follows a phased flow: first, it identifies suspicious emails containing prompt-injection keywords and baselines rare sign-ins to AI-integrated services. It then pivots to cloud audit logs to detect automated file access and outbound messaging performed by AI principals on behalf of users. By correlating the trigger email with subsequent automated activity, the hunt identifies successful agent-driven compromises.
